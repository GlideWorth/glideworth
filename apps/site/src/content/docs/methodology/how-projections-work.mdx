This page describes what the engine does, so you can judge the results. It is the same engine in the web app,
the API and the MCP server, and it is deterministic: the same inputs give the same projection.

## Time: one year at a time

The engine steps one calendar year at a time, or a custom fiscal or rolling year. New UK plans align to April,
Australian plans to July and New Zealand plans to April by default. A plan that starts in November has a short
first period of two months. Within a period, an event that starts in March counts for the months it is active:
amounts are **prorated by month**. There is no monthly or daily mode, so what happens inside a year (a crash in
March and a recovery by December) is not seen.

The plan starts in the month your balances are dated and runs to the last life-expectancy milestone, or the end you
set, never more than 100 years. The first and last years are labelled when they are partial.

## The pipeline

Before the first year, the engine:

1. Validates the input.
2. Chooses the starting finances (a snapshot plan uses its frozen finances, otherwise the live finances).
3. Merges plan overrides onto your accounts, assets and debts.
4. Resolves milestones that depend only on time (ages and dates). A circular definition is an error.
5. Computes the horizon.
6. Builds the periods from the year alignment.
7. Fixes the market rates for each period, then builds the price index.
8. Checks that every reference points at something that exists.

For each year, in order:

| Step | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Open | Ages and survival. Milestone effects (filing status, withholding) and deaths (survivor transfer, cost-basis step-up). |
| Time milestones | Milestones that resolve to this period are recorded. |
| Growth | Growth, dividends, interest and fees on the **opening** balance of each account, with bond allocation. |
| Income | Every income type: pensions, State Pension (with the triple lock), Social Security estimates, benefits, part-time and recurrence. |
| Real assets | Purchases, ownership costs, rent, loan amortisation, sales and appreciation. |
| Debts | Interest, scheduled payments and forgiveness. |
| Expenses | Expense events become required outflows. |
| Forced distributions | Required minimum distributions and similar are taken first, so they fund expenses first. |
| Spending strategy | The withdrawal rule, guardrails and flexible spending. |
| Tax strategy | Roth conversions and similar moves, within limits. |
| Settle | The cash-flow step below. |
| Close | Any unfunded shortfall is allocated, the ledger and totals are written, and milestones that depend on the end state are checked. |

### The settle step

Each year adds income, takes off tax and expenses, and sends any surplus down the **flows** in priority order. A
shortfall is drawn from accounts in the **drawdown** order. Tax depends on the withdrawals and the withdrawals depend
on the tax, so the engine repeats the calculation until the cash taxes change by less than a tiny tolerance, a
fraction of a penny. Tax is paid in the same year (the simplest) or settled in the next, with withholding in the year.

A metric milestone ("net worth at least ...") is reached in the last month of the year where it first holds, so
events tied to it start the **next** year.

## Real versus nominal, and flows versus stocks

Every amount you enter has a basis, real (today's money) or nominal (future money). All engine output is
nominal, with a price index for each year relative to the plan start. Any series can then be shown in today's money.

- **Flows** (income, expenses, taxes, contributions, withdrawals, shortfall) are valued at the price level when the
  year **starts**. So an amount entered in today's money, starting at the plan start, reads back exactly as entered
  in every year, including a partial first year. For example 2,300 a month plus 4,000 a year shows as 31,600 a year
  in today's money (an illustrative sum).
- **Stocks** (balances at the year end: net worth, account and loan balances) are valued at the price level at the
  year **end**.
- **Ratios** such as the savings rate and withdrawal rate are never adjusted for inflation.

Because flows and balances use different index points, the reconciliation for a year has an **Inflation** line: the
buying power the year's money lost between the start and the end of the year.

## Cash-flow timing

Growth is applied to the opening balance. Money added or taken out during the year earns part of the year's return,
set by the cash-flow timing assumption:

| Setting | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `start` | Flows happen at the start of the year and earn a full year's return. |
| `mid` (default) | Flows happen mid-year and earn half a year's return. |
| `end` | Flows happen at the end and earn none. |

Different tools choose differently, which is one reason two planners give different answers for the same inputs. It
changes how much growth a flow earns, not the price level it is valued at.

## How taxes plug in

Tax is computed per year by a stack of jurisdiction modules that depends on where you live. In estimate mode, a US
household in New York City gets federal, payroll, state and city; the UK, Canada, Australia, Germany, the Netherlands,
France, Spain, Israel, Ireland, New Zealand and the UAE each have their own module. Each level sees the output of the
earlier levels. Flat mode uses one rate per kind of income, and custom mode uses your own brackets.

Each module applies its own allowances, credits and rules to the income, gains and dividends of that year. Withdrawals
are taxed by account type: gains pro rata to cost basis for taxable accounts, ordinary income (less any tax-free share)
for pensions, contribution basis first for tax-free accounts. Statutory limits (contribution limits, access ages,
home-sale exclusions, minimum distributions) come from the account's country in every mode.

### What "estimate" means

A tax figure is an estimate. The tables are for stated tax years. Later years carry the last known rates forward and
index thresholds with inflation, which is wrong whenever the rules change. Each country's model leaves things out; the
list is shown with your tax results, and the main ones are on [Known limitations](/docs/methodology/known-limitations/).
The tables are checked against worked examples, not line by line against every official calculator.

## Reconciliation: "this adds up"

Every year's drill-down ends with a table showing that money in equals money out, that each account's opening balance
plus flows and growth equals its closing balance, and that last year's net worth plus the changes equals this year's.

<Callout type="note" title="What this does not do">
  The engine does not round intermediate values; rounding is only for display. It does not model events inside a year.
  Monthly resolution is reserved and currently rejected.
</Callout>
