Bank and card statement files give balances and transactions. Glideworth reconciles balances against your current
finances and summarises spending into annual expense events.

**Before you start:** a statement file from your bank in OFX, QFX (an OFX variant) or QIF format.

## Import it

<Steps>
1. Open **Import &amp; export** and choose the **Bank files** tab.
2. Drop the file. The format is detected, or you can choose it. QIF dates can need a date format.
3. Review the accounts found (account numbers are masked to their last four digits), the balance reconciliation, the
   annual spending summary and the issues.
4. Apply. Nothing is written until you do.
</Steps>

## How it works

- Balances are matched to your accounts by name, or by a mapping you give. The importer can create missing accounts and
  debts. A foreign-currency account keeps its currency. The preview asks for an exchange rate to your
  household currency before you apply, and saves it with your other rates. Without that rate the balance is left out
  of net worth and the plan cannot be simulated.
- Spending is summarised into annual expense events on the plan you have open (or the one you pick). An expense keeps
  the statement's currency. Transfers, income and card payments are excluded.
- Blocking errors change nothing.

Over the API, `POST /api/v1/households/{id}/import/bank-file` takes `text` and optional `format` (`ofx`, `qif` or
`auto`), `dateFormat`, `accountName`, `balances`, `planId`, `fxRates`, `annualise`, `categoryMap`, `excludeCategories` and `dryRun`.
`dryRun` defaults to `true`. `text` can be up to 2 MB (2,000,000 characters); for a larger file, export a shorter date
range. See [Import, export and reports](/docs/api-and-mcp/api-import-export-and-reports/).

## What this does not do

It does not connect to your bank, and it does not keep a transaction ledger. It reads a file you downloaded.
