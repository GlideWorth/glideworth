CSV import brings in three kinds of data. Every import starts as a dry run: you see what would change before anything is
written.

**Before you start:** a household, and a CSV file with a header row. Glideworth detects the delimiter, the column layout
and the number format.

## The three kinds

| Kind | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Account balances | Reconciles balances against your current finances: matched, created and unmatched accounts, with before and after balances. |
| Progress snapshots | Backfills your net-worth history. |
| Transactions | Summarises spending into annual expense events on a plan. |

## Presets

The importer recognises these layouts. It shows "Detected: ..." and you can pick a preset or choose none.

- **Transactions:** YNAB register export, Monarch Money, Mint, Copilot Money, Actual Budget, Monzo, Starling Bank, and a
  UK bank statement with Debit and Credit columns.
- **Account balances:** a generic layout (Date, Account, Balance) and Glideworth's own account export.
- **Progress snapshots:** a generic net-worth tracker (Date, Net Worth) and Glideworth's own progress export.

A file that matches no preset can still be imported by mapping columns by hand.

## Import it

<Steps>
1. Open **Import &amp; export** and choose the **CSV files** tab (or "Import CSV files" on the Welcome page).
2. Choose the kind of data and drop the file.
3. Check the detected preset and columns, and the issues list.
4. For balances, map unmatched accounts to your accounts, or let the importer create missing ones.
5. Review the before and after reconciliation, then apply. Finances, snapshots and the plan are written in one
   transaction. Blocking errors change nothing.
</Steps>

Over the API, `POST /api/v1/households/{id}/import/csv` takes `text`, `kind`, optional `options` (such as `accountMap`
and `createMissing`), `planId` and `dryRun`. `dryRun` defaults to `true`. `kind` is `account-balances`,
`progress-snapshots` or `transactions`. See [Import, export and reports](/docs/api-and-mcp/api-import-export-and-reports/).

## What this does not do

- It does not sync. A CSV is a one-off import. Direct sync with budgeting apps is not built.
- Transactions become annual expense estimates, not a transaction ledger. Transfers, income and card payments are excluded.
- It does not guess currencies: foreign-currency accounts keep their currency and need an exchange rate.
