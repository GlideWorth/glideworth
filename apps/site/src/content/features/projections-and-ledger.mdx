A projection takes your plan and your assumptions and walks forward one year at a time. It is deterministic:
the same inputs always give the same result, using one set of assumptions such as a fixed growth rate and
inflation. It is the baseline that [Monte Carlo](/features/monte-carlo/) then stresses with many market
histories.

## What you get

For each year: income by source, tax by jurisdiction and kind, spending, contributions, withdrawals by account,
investment returns, and the balance of every account and asset. Charts show net worth stacked by category,
and tables export as JSON or CSV (year by year, cash flow, taxes and account balances). A cash-flow view shows one
year's money moving from sources to uses.

Each year can be opened as a **drill-down**, and the drill-down ends with a reconciliation: money in equals money
out, each account's opening balance plus its flows and growth equals its closing balance, and last year's net
worth plus this year's changes equals this year's. If a number cannot be traced to the ledger, that is a bug,
and we want to hear about it.

## How a year is calculated

- **Income and expenses first.** Each period adds income, takes off tax and expenses, and the surplus goes down
  your [flows](/features/plan-modelling/) in priority order. A shortfall is drawn from accounts in your drawdown
  order (by default cash, then taxable investments, then crypto, then tax-deferred, HSA and tax-free accounts),
  which you can reorder and set start dates for.
- **Tax and withdrawals depend on each other.** Withdrawals create tax, and tax creates a bigger shortfall, so
  the engine repeats the calculation until the two agree.
- **Growth** applies to the opening balance. Money added or taken out during the year earns part of the year's
  return according to a cash-flow timing setting: mid-year by default (half a year's return), or start or end.
  Different planners choose differently, which is one reason two planners can disagree on identical inputs.
- **Tax can be paid in the same year** (the simplest) **or settled in the next**, like tax withheld during a year
  with the balance paid after.

## Today's money and future money

Every amount you enter says which it is. The engine calculates in nominal money with a price index for each
period, so any series can be shown in today's money or in the money of the year. The toggle is only a change of
view; it does not change the plan. Flows deflate by the index at the start of the period and balances by the
index at the end, and the year drill-down shows an **Inflation** line for the buying power that a year's money
lost in between, so every figure matches the one shown elsewhere.

## Partial years are labelled

A plan starts in the month your balances are dated and ends at its horizon, so the first and last years are
often shorter than twelve months. Their income and spending look low next to full years, so they are labelled
(for example 2026\*) and can be shown annualised. Balances, rates, multi-year totals and CSV exports always use
the actual months.

## Warnings

The projection flags what you would want to know about: a year when spending is not fully funded, an early
withdrawal that carries a penalty, a milestone that is never reached. The [assumption audit](/features/explanations-and-audit/)
checks for modelling mistakes, such as counting housing twice or mixing today's and future money.

## What it does not do

- **Annual steps only.** Monthly or daily resolution is reserved in the schema and rejected today. Sequence
  effects within a year are not seen.
- **One path is not a forecast.** A deterministic run uses one set of assumptions. For how fragile the plan is,
  use [Monte Carlo](/features/monte-carlo/).
- **Law changes are your assumption.** Rules are as stated for the tax years in the tables, plus what is already
  legislated. See [tax estimates](/features/tax-estimates/).
- **Behaviour is not modelled:** panic selling, windfalls you have not planned, a marriage or divorce you have
  not entered.
- **No "results changed because" note yet.** After a tax-table or engine update, results can change; a report
  saying why is planned, not built. Results are cached per plan version and engine version, so a stale number is
  never served after an update.
- **Reports are a printable view** produced by your browser. Server-side PDF is not built.
