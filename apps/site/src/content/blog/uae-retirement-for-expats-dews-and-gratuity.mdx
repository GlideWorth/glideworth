If you are an expatriate working in the United Arab Emirates, there is no UAE state pension for you: the federal pension
system run by the General Pension and Social Security Authority (GPSSA) is for UAE nationals. What you build up at work
is an end-of-service benefit. In most private-sector jobs that is the **gratuity** under Article 51 of the Labour Law,
a lump sum of 21 or 30 days of basic wage for each year of service. In the Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC)
it is **DEWS**, a savings plan your employer pays into every month, and elsewhere your employer may have joined the
federal **savings scheme** that works the same way. Anything beyond that, your retirement plan in the UAE is the money
you save yourself.

This article explains each of the three, compares the gratuity with DEWS over eight years, and then covers what UK
expatriates usually ask next: National Insurance while living in Dubai or Abu Dhabi, and transfers of UK pensions to
overseas schemes (QROPS).

  contract, your employer's scheme and your own circumstances decide what you receive. See the

</Callout>

## Is there a pension in Dubai for expatriates?

No state pension. The UAE government portal describes GPSSA registration as mandatory for UAE nationals working in the
government and private sectors; citizens of other Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) countries stay in their home country's
scheme through the GCC protection extension programme
([UAE government portal: pensions and social security for UAE citizens](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/Sector-of-employment/working-in-uae-government-sector/pensions-and-social-security-for-uae-citizens)).
Expatriates are not part of it, so a "pension plan in the UAE" for an expatriate means one of the following:

1. the end-of-service gratuity, paid by your employer when you leave;
2. DEWS or the federal savings scheme, if your employer is in one, which replaces the gratuity;
3. your own savings and investments, in the UAE or elsewhere, and any pension you built in another country.

## The end-of-service gratuity (Article 51)

For an expatriate employee in the private sector, the UAE government portal sets out the rule from Article 51 of the
Labour Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021)
([UAE government portal: end-of-service benefits](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/Sector-of-employment/employment-in-the-private-sector/end-of-service-benefits-for-employees-in-the-private-sector)):

| Rule | What the portal says |
| --- | --- |
| Minimum service | At least 1 year of continuous service; nothing below that |
| Years 1 to 5 | 21 days of wage for each year |
| Each year after 5 | 30 days of wage for each year |
| Part years | Pro rata, once the first year is complete |
| Which wage | The basic wage, without allowances such as housing, transport, utilities or furniture |
| Unpaid absence | Not counted as service |
| Cap | The total may not exceed the wage of 2 years |
| When it is paid | With your final wages, within 14 days of the end of employment |

Two things are worth knowing. First, the gratuity is calculated on your **last** basic wage, so pay rises late in your
service raise the whole benefit. Second, it depends on your employer still being there and able to pay when you leave.
The portal gives the rule in days of wage but no formula for the daily wage; dividing the monthly basic wage by 30, or
the yearly basic wage by 365, gives slightly different results. The
[UAE gratuity calculator](/tools/uae-gratuity-calculator/) shows the calculation tier by tier.

UAE nationals in the private sector follow the pension legislation instead, the portal says, not Article 51.

## DEWS: the DIFC Employee Workplace Savings plan

DEWS is the DIFC's own replacement for the gratuity. The DIFC describes it as restructuring the end-of-service benefit
into "a funded and professionally managed, defined contribution plan", with a voluntary savings option, a choice of
investments for different risk appetites and Sharia-compliant options
([DIFC: Qualifying Schemes](https://www.difc.com/business/qualifying-schemes)). It was introduced in the DIFC from
1 February 2020 ([Dubai Media Office, 21 January 2020](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2020/jan/21-01/difc-unveils-new-progressive-employee-workplace-savings-plan)).

### Who has to be in it

Under Article 66 of the DIFC Employment Law (DIFC Law No. 2 of 2019, consolidated version of July 2025), a DIFC employer
must pay into a **Qualifying Scheme** every month for each employee who is not an "Exempted Employee"
([DIFC Employment Law](https://assets.difc.com/v1/media/edge/images/dubaiintern0078-difcexperie96c5-production-3253/media/project/difcexperiences/difc/difcwebsite/documents/laws--regulations/employment-law.pdf),
Article 66(7) and Schedule 1). DEWS is the default Qualifying Scheme; an employer may use another scheme that holds a
DIFC Certificate of Compliance (Article 66(9) and (10)). Exempted employees include UAE and GCC nationals who must be
registered with the GPSSA (Article 65) and, to the extent of their profit drawings, equity partners.

The employer must register you within two months of your scheme start date, and may defer contributions during an
agreed probation period, paying them back to the start of your employment once you are confirmed (Article 66(11) and
(12)).

### How much the employer pays

| Service | Employer contribution (Article 66(7)) |
| --- | --- |
| First 5 years | 5.83% of monthly basic wage |
| Each year after 5 | 8.33% of monthly basic wage |

For this, the basic wage cannot be counted as less than 50% of your monthly wage (Article 66(8)(b)). Contributions for
each month are due by the 21st day of the next month (Article 66(17)). The rates are not arbitrary: 21 days is about
5.83% of a 360-day year and 30 days about 8.33%, so the contributions track the old gratuity, paid month by month on
the pay you earn at the time rather than all at once on your final pay. Service in the DIFC before your scheme start
date still earns a gratuity under the DIFC rules for that period (Article 66(1) to (4)).

### Voluntary contributions and investment choice

You can ask your employer in writing to deduct part of your pay and pay it into the scheme (Article 66(14)). The law
sets no cap, and the DIFC's page publishes none. The investment risk on the money paid in is yours (Article 66(15)).
The scheme administrator, Zurich Workplace Solutions, says money is placed in its "Low/Moderate Growth" default fund
unless you choose otherwise, and that you can take up to two partial withdrawals a year of up to 30% of your voluntary
savings while still employed ([Zurich Workplace Solutions: DEWS for employees](https://zws.zurich.ae/dews/employee)).

### When you get the money

The employer-funded part is an end-of-service benefit: you can reach it only once you leave that employment. The
administrator's page says that after leaving you can withdraw part or all of it as cash, or stay invested, including
after you have left Dubai and the UAE ([Zurich Workplace Solutions](https://zws.zurich.ae/dews/employee)). Because the
money sits in a trust rather than with your employer, it does not depend on your employer being able to pay on your
last day.

### DEWS for Dubai Government employees

Searches for "DEWS" also bring up a separate scheme for foreign employees of Dubai Government entities, the Savings
Scheme for Foreign Employees in the Government of Dubai, which Zurich also administers under the DEWS name. The Dubai
Media Office announced that enrolment is by default for foreign employees, phased by job grade; the employer pays in
the end-of-service gratuity due under the HR legislation; employees may add their own savings; and on leaving they can
withdraw the whole amount or stay in the scheme
([Dubai Media Office, 15 March 2022](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2022/march/15-03/the-steering-committee-of-the-savings-scheme-for-foreign-employees-in-the-government-of-dubai)).

## The federal savings scheme (Cabinet Resolution No. 96 of 2023)

Outside the DIFC, employers in the private sector and free zones can choose the voluntary alternative end-of-service
benefits scheme under Cabinet Resolution No. 96 of 2023
([UAE government portal: end-of-service benefits](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/Sector-of-employment/employment-in-the-private-sector/end-of-service-benefits-for-employees-in-the-private-sector)).
It is optional for the employer, not for you: an employer can enrol all staff or particular groups, applies to the
Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MoHRE) and contracts with a fund approved by the Securities and
Commodities Authority. Once you are enrolled, the gratuity stops accruing, and the gratuity already earned must be
settled.

| Feature | What the portal says |
| --- | --- |
| Employer contribution, under 5 years' service | 5.83% of monthly basic salary |
| Employer contribution, more than 5 years' service | 8.33% of monthly basic salary |
| When the employer pays | Within 15 days of the start of each month |
| Your own top-ups | Optional, by payroll deduction or transfer, up to 25% of your total wage |
| Investment options | A capital-guaranteed portfolio (aimed at unskilled workers), risk-based portfolios and Sharia-compliant funds |
| When you get it | Contributions and returns within 14 days of the end of employment; you can also leave the money invested and withdraw at any time |

## Gratuity or DEWS: a worked example over 8 years

<Callout type="note" title="Illustrative">
  Made-up round figures to show how the two rules behave. They are not a forecast or advice. DEWS and the federal
  savings scheme use the same employer rates, so the scheme column applies to both. The gratuity uses a daily wage of
  monthly basic ÷ 30.
</Callout>

An employee starts on a monthly basic wage of AED 20,000 and leaves after exactly 8 years. Two pay paths: basic wage
flat at AED 20,000, or rising 3% a year (AED 24,597 in the eighth year). The scheme contributions are paid at the end of
each month and earn either nothing or a steady 4% a year after fees.

**Gratuity, flat pay:** 5 × 21 + 3 × 30 = 195 days. AED 20,000 ÷ 30 = AED 666.67 a day, so 195 × 666.67 = **AED 130,000**.
The cap (24 × AED 20,000 = AED 480,000) is not reached.

**Scheme, flat pay:** 60 months × 5.83% × AED 20,000 (AED 1,166) = AED 69,960, plus 36 months × 8.33% × AED 20,000
(AED 1,666) = AED 59,976, so **AED 129,936** paid in.

| Pay path | Gratuity | Scheme, 0% return | Scheme, 4% a year |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Flat at AED 20,000 | AED 130,000 | AED 129,936 | AED 150,343 |
| Rising 3% a year | AED 159,884 | AED 145,921 | AED 167,790 |

What the table shows:

- With no investment return the two are almost the same on flat pay; the small gap is 5.83% being a rounded 21/360.
- Pay rises favour the gratuity, because it is paid on the **final** wage for every year of service, while the scheme
  is paid on the wage at the time.
- Investment return favours the scheme. Returns can also be negative, and that risk is yours.
- The scheme money is held outside your employer, and you can usually leave it invested after you leave.

## UK expatriates: National Insurance while you live in the UAE

The UK has social security agreements with a list of countries, and the United Arab Emirates is not one of them
([HMRC: Social security abroad, NI38](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/social-security-abroad-ni38/guidance-on-social-security-abroad-ni38),
updated 7 July 2026). So the agreement rules that let some workers abroad keep paying UK National Insurance do not
apply in the UAE. Years you spend in the UAE without UK contributions can leave gaps in your National Insurance record,
and the UK State Pension depends on that record. You can check your record and forecast on
[gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/check-state-pension).

The way to fill gaps from abroad is voluntary contributions, and the rules changed from the 2026 to 2027 tax year
([HMRC: Voluntary National Insurance contributions abroad from 6 April 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/voluntary-national-insurance-contributions-for-periods-abroad-from-6-april-2026/voluntary-national-insurance-contributions-abroad-from-6-april-2026),
published 16 March 2026):

- **Class 2 for periods abroad is closed from 6 April 2026**, except for people treated as self-employed in the UK
  under a social security agreement and volunteer development workers. With no UK–UAE agreement, the first exception
  does not cover work in the UAE.
- **Class 3 for periods abroad has a stricter test for new applicants.** You need to have lived in the UK for at least
  10 continuous years, or to have built at least 10 qualifying years on your National Insurance record; voluntary
  contributions paid for periods abroad do not count towards those 10 years, with narrow exceptions. NI38 also lists
  a third route: having paid Class 1 contributions for the first 52 weeks of employment abroad. For periods before
  6 April 2026 the residence test was 3 continuous years.
- **Existing Class 3 payers abroad** can carry on without reapplying.
- **Former Class 2 payers** who meet the transitional conditions can apply for Class 3 under the old 3-year test if
  they apply before 6 April 2027. HMRC said it would contact them in July 2026.
- **Earlier years are not affected:** you can still pay Class 2 or Class 3 for tax years before 2026 to 2027 under the
  rules for those years.

The rates for 2026 to 2027 are **£18.40 a week for Class 3** and £3.65 a week for Class 2
([gov.uk: Voluntary National Insurance rates](https://www.gov.uk/voluntary-national-insurance-contributions/rates)).
A full year of Class 3 at that rate is 52 × £18.40 = £956.80. For the previous two tax years, Class 3 is charged at the
rate that applied in each of those years; for earlier years, gov.uk says you pay the 2026 to 2027 rate.

## QROPS and UK pension transfers to the UAE

A QROPS (qualifying recognised overseas pension scheme) is, in HMRC's words, "a pension scheme established outside the
UK that is broadly similar to a UK registered pension scheme"
([HMRC: The overseas transfer charge guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/qualifying-recognised-overseas-pension-schemes-charge-on-transfers/the-overseas-transfer-charge-guidance),
paragraph 1.1). The facts worth knowing before anyone talks to you about one:

- **HMRC publishes a list of schemes that have told it they meet the conditions** to be a recognised overseas pension
  scheme (ROPS), updated on the 1st and 15th of each month. HMRC says: "We cannot guarantee these are ROPS or that any
  transfers to them will be free of UK tax" ([HMRC: ROPS notification list](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-the-recognised-overseas-pension-schemes-notification-list),
  updated 16 November 2026).
- **A transfer can carry an overseas transfer charge of 25%** of the amount transferred, unless an exclusion applies
  ([gov.uk: Overseas pensions, pension transfers](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/overseas-pensions-pension-transfers)).
  The charge can still arise if circumstances change within 5 full tax years of the transfer.
- **The exclusion for transfers to schemes in the EEA and Gibraltar was removed** for transfers made on or after
  30 October 2024 ([HMRC: Reducing tax-free overseas transfers of tax relieved UK pensions](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/changes-to-rules-for-overseas-pensions-and-scheme-administrators/reducing-tax-free-overseas-transfers-of-tax-relieved-uk-pensions),
  published 30 October 2024), and from 6 April 2025 schemes in the EEA must meet the same conditions as schemes
  elsewhere.

Whether a transfer suits you is a question for a regulated adviser; HMRC's guidance is the place to check the rules.

## Planning with this in Glideworth

The [UAE planning page](/planning/ae/) sets out the model in full. The UAE tax line is always zero, because the UAE
levies no personal income tax. You can set an end-of-service gratuity on a salary, with the share of the salary that is
basic wage, and it is paid when the salary ends, using the Article 51 tiers, the two-year cap on basic wage and a daily
wage of monthly basic ÷ 30. DEWS and the federal savings scheme are account types: you enter the employer contributions
as a flow into the account, and do not also set a gratuity on that salary. GPSSA pensions for UAE nationals are
modelled at a basic level, and the dirham's peg to the US dollar (3.6725) is pre-filled as an exchange rate. Glideworth
does not model the gratuity reductions under the old law, unpaid leave days or limited-contract rules.

If you have a UK State Pension, you can add it to the plan as a state pension income from the UK, with the amount from
your forecast. Glideworth's UK model leaves out voluntary Class 2 and Class 3 contributions, so it does not work out
your National Insurance record or what voluntary years would add; if you pay them, enter the cost as an expense and the
higher forecast as the pension amount.
