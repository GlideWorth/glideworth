The engine models residency changes, tax on income where it arises for non-residents, and treaty relief.
The app's editing screens cover the timeline, per-person details and item countries, and a "Stay or move?"
comparison.

**Before you start:** a plan, with a country set on any account, property, debt or income that sits abroad.

## How it works

- **Where you live, over time.** Your household's country is where you live at the start. Add moves to the plan's
  residency timeline (a month, a country and region, and one person or both), or put a relocation effect on a
  milestone. Partners can live in different countries. A move part-way through a tax year splits it by months.
- **Where each thing is.** Every account, property, debt and income has a country. It defaults to the household's
  country (or the account type's: an ISA is British), and it does not change when you move.
- **Which country may tax what.** The country you live in taxes worldwide income. A country where something is
  may also tax it as source (rent and property gains almost everywhere). A treaty table says who keeps the right to
  tax each kind of income, and your country of residence gives a credit for tax paid elsewhere. US citizens and
  green-card holders also pay US tax wherever they live, after the foreign earned income exclusion and a credit.

## Add a move

<Steps>
1. Open the **Plan editor** and choose **Where you live**. Each person's residence is a strip over the plan.
2. Add a dated move: who, when, and where (country and region). Or add a "Relocate" effect on a milestone.
3. Read the warnings: countries without rules, unknown treaty pairs and missing exchange rates (with an inline box
   to add the rate).
4. In **Current finances**, open **About you**, then "Citizenship, past residence and state pension" to add
   citizenships, a US green card, where you lived before the plan and state-pension records.
5. Open **Taxes** to see tax by country and by reason, and "How this is taxed, and why" for any item.
</Steps>

## Stay or move?

Open **Insights** and choose **Stay or move?**. Pick where, when, who and whether you come back ("Move to",
"From", "Back in"). The comparison runs two deterministic projections and shows lifetime tax by country for both,
where the difference in end net worth comes from, the plan changes, and buttons to save it as a branch or compare.
For a chance-of-success comparison, branch the move and run [Chance of success](/docs/planning/read-your-results/) on both.

## What changes when you move

- **Wrappers.** An ISA keeps its UK shelter but you cannot pay in while you live abroad. UK pension contributions
  get relief on up to 3,600 pounds a year for five tax years after leaving. Canadian TFSA and RRSP contributions
  stop. A new country may not recognise a foreign tax-free account; by default the growth is taxed on withdrawal.
- **Exit and arrival.** Leaving Canada or Australia triggers a deemed sale (you can defer it); arriving resets cost
  to market value. Returning to the UK within five years makes gains realised abroad taxable in the year you return.
- **State pensions.** A UK State Pension is frozen where it is not uprated, and returns to the current rate if you
  come back. The audit shows years short and the cost of voluntary contributions.
- **Inheritance tax.** After leaving the UK you stay in scope of UK inheritance tax on your worldwide estate for 3 to
  10 years, depending on how long you lived there.

The United Arab Emirates is covered: no personal income, capital gains, dividend, interest, wealth or inheritance
tax for individuals, the end-of-service gratuity, DEWS and the federal savings scheme as accounts.

<Callout type="risk" title="What this does not do">
  It applies no residence test: you are resident where your timeline says (no statutory residence test, no 183-day
  rule or tie-breaker). Split years are prorated by months. Treaty relief is assumed claimed. Only UK-UAE is
  implemented article by article; UK-US, UK-Australia, UK-Canada, UK-Spain, UK-France and US-Canada are basic;
  other pairs use the credit method with a warning. Departure taxes cover taxable investment accounts only. Not
  tax advice.
</Callout>
