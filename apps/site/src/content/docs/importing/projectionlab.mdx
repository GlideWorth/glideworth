Glideworth reads a ProjectionLab JSON export and builds a household, its current finances, every plan and your
net-worth history. Nothing is lost silently: every field is classified and the import report lists everything that was
dropped, approximated or could not be mapped.

Still deciding whether to move? [Switch from ProjectionLab](/compare/switch-from-projectionlab/) covers what comes
across, what you gain and what you give up.

**Before you start:** a ProjectionLab data export, and Glideworth running. The format checked is ProjectionLab export
version 4.6.0. A newer version imports too, with keys the importer does not recognise reported.

## Import it

<Steps>
1. In ProjectionLab, open your account menu and export your data as JSON.
2. In Glideworth, open **Import &amp; export** and choose the **From ProjectionLab** tab (or choose "Import from
   ProjectionLab" on the Welcome page).
3. Choose the file ("Choose your ProjectionLab export", up to 25 MB). It is read and converted in your browser;
   nothing is uploaded while you review.
4. Review the counts and the full report.
5. Choose Import. Only the converted plan is saved, to your own server.
</Steps>

Over the API, run a dry run first, then repeat without `dryRun` (see [Import, export and reports](/docs/api-and-mcp/api-import-export-and-reports/)):

```bash
curl -s -X POST $GLIDEWORTH/api/v1/import -H "Authorization: Bearer $KEY" -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d "{\"format\":\"projectionlab\",\"dryRun\":true,\"data\":$(cat projectionlab-export.json)}"
```

## The import report

The report lists every issue by key path in your file (such as `$.plans[0].income.events[2].amount`) and never repeats
your values. Each issue has a severity (info, warning, error) and a category:

| Category | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| dropped | Intentionally not carried over: interface state, derived values, redundant copies, secrets. |
| approximated | Carried over, but Glideworth's model differs; the message says how. Check these by hand. |
| unsupported | Real planning content with no home in the schema yet. |
| unrecognised | A key the importer does not know, such as from a newer export version. |
| defaulted, invalid, info | A default was chosen, a value was invalid, or something worth knowing. |

For the version-4.6 format, the importer classifies 1,135 key paths: 748 mapped, 387 ignored (interface state, derived
values, copies, secrets) and none unsupported or unknown.

## What maps exactly

People, location and currency; accounts, property and debts with balances, owners, growth, dividends, fees and
withdrawal rules; every plan's income, expenses, milestones, savings priorities, withdrawal strategy, tax settings and
Monte Carlo settings; and net-worth history. Conversions: percentages are divided by 100; "today$" amounts become real
and "$" amounts nominal; ProjectionLab dates become months; owners become the primary person, the partner or joint;
ids are kept when they fit and otherwise slugged.

## What is approximated

These are reported as approximated:

- A Move milestone is kept as a marker with no effect. Glideworth does not model moving country, so its destination is
  ignored and reported. The keys for it are inferred, because the checked export carried none.
- Withdraw and liquid values such as "after a given age" map to Glideworth's rules for when an account can be drawn.
- Rental management fees: ProjectionLab applies the fee to rental income, Glideworth to asset value. A rental income
  given as an amount is read as per year.
- Priorities: "contributions are fixed" becomes mandatory, target-mode priorities become a target strategy, and an
  asset priority becomes a pay-down of the asset's loan.
- Monte Carlo: ProjectionLab's random-restart backtest becomes historical random restart, and per-series means and
  standard deviations become normal distributions. A fixed seed is kept.
- Tax: with tax estimation on and a supported country the mode is estimate, otherwise flat, or custom brackets where the
  source uses them.
- Progress dates are rounded to the nearest day.

## What has no equivalent

- The `non-qualified-uk-pension` drawdown entry is dropped, because Glideworth models early pension access per account.
- Debts with a payment frequency other than monthly: payments are monthly, and other values are reported.
- A per-plan override of an asset's loan or value cannot be represented: Glideworth cannot override a loan per plan. The
  difference is reported.
- Starting conditions other than the default (live finances).
- Your ProjectionLab plugin API key. It is never read into the document or the report.

## Re-import

Importing again into the same household needs `"mode": "replace"` (over the API) or a choice in the app. The three modes
are `create` (the default; `409` if the household exists), `replace` (the household becomes the document; plans and
snapshots not in it are removed) and `merge` (the household, finances and the plans in the document are updated;
everything else is kept). Plan version history is preserved: an existing plan keeps its earlier versions and the import
is recorded as its next version.

<Callout type="warning" title="Check what changed">
  Anything marked approximated or unsupported must be checked by hand. An import is a starting point, not proof that two
  tools agree: they calculate differently (for example, cash-flow timing).
</Callout>
