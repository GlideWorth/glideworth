If you have decided to leave ProjectionLab, or want to see whether your plan holds up somewhere else, this page
covers how to bring the plan across, what arrives intact and what you will need to check by hand. It also covers
what you gain, what you give up, and how to try Glideworth before you pay anything.

For a side-by-side look at the two products, read the [ProjectionLab alternative](/compare/projectionlab/)
comparison instead.

## Bring your plan across

Glideworth imports the JSON backup file ProjectionLab lets you download. The file is converted in your browser, and
you see a report of what was carried over before anything is saved.

<Steps>
1. **Download your ProjectionLab backup.** ProjectionLab's help says to go to **Account Settings &gt; Data &gt;
   Export Data** and choose **Export**. The file holds your plans, Net Worth, history and settings
   ([ProjectionLab help](https://projectionlab.com/help/back-up-and-restore)). Keep it private: it is your whole
   financial picture.
2. **Open Glideworth.** Start a free trial, or run your own copy. Open **Import &amp; export** and choose the
   **From ProjectionLab** tab, or choose "Import from ProjectionLab" on the Welcome page.
3. **Choose the file** (up to 25 MB). It is read and converted in your browser, and nothing is uploaded while you
   review.
4. **Read the report.** It shows counts of what was imported and lists every field that was dropped, approximated
   or not recognised, by its place in your file. It never repeats your amounts.
5. **Import.** If you already have data in Glideworth, choose whether the import replaces it or is merged into it.
6. **Work through the checklist.** Glideworth then gives you a short checklist: people, location and currency;
   account balances; each plan; the parts of the report that need a look; and a first chance-of-success run on
   your main plan.
</Steps>

For the field-by-field detail, see how to [import ProjectionLab JSON](/docs/importing/projectionlab/).

### Which ProjectionLab version the importer reads

The importer was built and tested against ProjectionLab's version 4.6 export, where it accounts for every one of
the 1,135 key paths in the file. ProjectionLab released version 5.0.0 on 20 November 2026
([changelog](https://projectionlab.com/changelog)). A version 5 backup still imports: the report warns that the
version is newer than the importer's and lists every key it does not recognise, so nothing is dropped without a
line in the report. We have not yet tested the importer against a version 5 backup, so read that report with
care. If you downloaded a backup before you moved to version 5, that file is in the format the importer was tested
against.

### What comes across

- People, where you live, and your currency.
- Accounts, property and debts, with balances, owners, growth, dividends, fees and withdrawal rules.
- Every plan: income, expenses, milestones, savings priorities, the withdrawal strategy, tax settings and Monte
  Carlo settings.
- Your net-worth history.

Amounts in today's dollars stay in today's dollars, and future-dollar amounts stay as future dollars.

### What does not, or not exactly

The report marks each of these, so you know where to look:

- **Approximations.** Some ProjectionLab settings have no exact match and are converted, with a note saying how.
  For example, rental management fees, "after a given age" withdrawal rules, savings priorities in target mode,
  and Monte Carlo return settings.
- **No equivalent yet.** Starting conditions other than the default one, a per-plan override of an asset's loan
  or value, and debt payments that are not monthly.
- **Left out on purpose.** Chart and table preferences, layout and other interface settings, billing state, and
  your ProjectionLab plugin API key, which is never read into the plan or the report.
- **Results.** Glideworth runs its own engine and tax tables, so even identical inputs give different projected
  figures. Compare the two before you rely on either.

### Per-account balance history

ProjectionLab version 5 can export your balance history as a CSV file from its history tools
([ProjectionLab help](https://projectionlab.com/help/import-or-export-balance-history)). Glideworth can import a
balance history from a CSV or TSV file or from cells pasted from a spreadsheet: you match each column to an
account, check every cell in a preview, then merge or replace. See
[Import balance history](/docs/importing/balance-history/). We have not tested it with a ProjectionLab history
file specifically.

## What ProjectionLab users asked for, already in Glideworth

ProjectionLab collected feature requests on a public roadmap board, Changemap. Its launch post for version 5 says
it has saved that feedback and is retiring the board
([launch post](https://projectionlab.com/blog/meet-the-new-projectionlab)), and the request pages no longer open.
The vote counts below are as we read them on the date shown.

- **What-if scenarios that follow your main plan.** In Glideworth a what-if is a branch: it keeps only its
  differences, so when you update the main plan, the branch picks up the change. You compare them side by side
  and merge one back if you like it. ProjectionLab's request for a "branching plan model with variables shared
  with parent plan" had 275 votes
  ([roadmap](https://changemap.co/projectifi/projectifi/task/6243-branching-plan-model-with-variables/), read
  19 Nov 2026). See [branches and change sets](/features/branches-and-change-sets/).
- **One household, two sign-ins.** Your partner gets their own sign-in as an editor, and an adviser can be a
  read-only viewer. The "family account" request had 249 votes
  ([roadmap](https://changemap.co/projectifi/projectifi/task/6249-family-account/), read 19 Nov 2026). See
  [sharing and roles](/features/sharing-and-roles/).
- **Spending guardrails based on risk.** Glideworth shows what spending its guardrails allow this year and the
  portfolio level at which they would cut it. "Risk-based withdrawal guardrails" had 137 votes
  ([roadmap](https://changemap.co/projectifi/projectifi/task/9189-risk-based-withdrawal-guardrails/), read
  19 Nov 2026). See [withdrawal strategies and guardrails](/features/withdrawal-strategies/).
- **Talk to ChatGPT or Claude about your plan.** Connect your assistant and ask why the plan fails in a bad
  decade, or what retiring two years earlier would cost. Any change it suggests is a proposal you approve. "MCP
  server for LLM integration" had 89 votes
  ([roadmap](https://changemap.co/projectifi/projectifi/task/11092-mcp-server-for-llm-integration/), read
  23 Nov 2026). See [ChatGPT and Claude on your plan](/features/mcp-server/).
- **Several currencies.** Accounts, property, debts and income can each keep their own currency, with exchange
  rates you set and can stress-test. See [multi-currency planning](/features/multi-currency/).

## If version 5 changed your plan

ProjectionLab's own transition guide lists what to check after the update
([v5 Transition Guide](https://projectionlab.com/help/projectionlab-v5-early-access-guide), updated 24 Nov 2026).
Among other things, it says:

- Accounts that existed in only one plan "could not be carried over automatically", and "any related
  contributions or transfers were removed too". Once you select **Got it**, the list of affected accounts "cannot
  be reopened".
- Version 4 stored progress as category totals, so ProjectionLab estimated the history of each account from those
  totals, and the guide asks you to review the estimates.
- Starting Conditions no longer has its own page, and version 5 requires a main plan.
- Liquid, qualified-age and penalty settings now come from Net Worth and apply across plans by default.
- "Plugins and integrations may need to be updated for this release."

If you are reviewing every plan anyway, that is a reasonable moment to import a copy into Glideworth and compare.
Glideworth's import report tells you what it changed, field by field, before you save.

## No lock-in

- **Everything exports.** Your whole household comes out as a Glideworth JSON file you can import again, and the
  projection tables come out as CSV.
- **The code is open source** (AGPL-3.0). If the company behind Glideworth disappeared, you or anyone could keep
  running it.
- **Self-hosting is free, with every feature.** For technical users: one Docker container or one Node process,
  your data in a single file on your own machine. No licence key, and nothing is switched off. See
  [self-hosting](/self-hosting/).

On ProjectionLab's side: in March 2025 it decided to release future updates to its web app only, and bug-fix
support for self-hosted copies ran until 31 December 2025. Existing self-hosted copies "will remain operational
indefinitely" ([self-hosting update](https://projectionlab.com/help/offline-version)). Its pricing page lists
Basic, Premium and Pro plans and no self-hosted plan ([pricing](https://projectionlab.com/pricing)).

## Money in more than one country

If you live in the US but still have, say, a UK pension or a flat in London, Glideworth taxes each item where it
sits as well as where you live. Accounts, property and income in another country are taxed there as a non-resident,
and the country you live in gives credit for that tax under its treaty. The tax results show tax by country and
why, and any item can be explained with the rule behind it.

The household lives in one country for the whole plan. See
[assets and income in another country](/docs/planning/tax-and-currency/#assets-and-income-in-another-country) for
the treaty pairs and countries covered, and what is not modelled.

ProjectionLab's home page offers "a growing list of international tax presets and account types, including Canada,
the United Kingdom, Australia, Germany, the Netherlands, and more" ([home page](https://projectionlab.com/)). We
found nothing on its public pages about how it taxes assets held in a country you do not live in; that does not
mean it has no such feature.

## Where ProjectionLab is still better

- **Maturity.** ProjectionLab has been developed since 2021 ([about](https://projectionlab.com/about), read
  16 Nov 2026) and has a release history and community channels. Glideworth is new.
- **Tax optimisation.** ProjectionLab's Optimize tool, added in version 4.6.0, targets federal brackets, IRMAA,
  ACA limits and the NIIT cliff ([changelog](https://projectionlab.com/changelog), read 16 Nov 2026). Glideworth
  models Roth conversions, IRMAA and the ACA credit, but its optimiser is less developed.
- **Advisers.** ProjectionLab has a Pro plan for advisers and coaches who manage clients
  ([pricing](https://projectionlab.com/pricing)). Glideworth has no adviser tier.
- **A free hosted tier.** ProjectionLab's Basic plan is free ([pricing](https://projectionlab.com/pricing)).
  Glideworth's hosted service is paid after the trial; only self-hosting is free.
