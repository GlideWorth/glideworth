## 1. Subject matter and duration

Glideworth processes personal data for the customer to provide the hosted service described in the terms. The
details are in Annex I. This agreement lasts as long as Glideworth processes that data, which includes the
period before deleted data ages out of backups.

## 2. Instructions

Glideworth will process the personal data only on the customer's documented instructions. The terms, this
agreement, and the customer's use of the service's features (including the API, MCP server, export and deletion)
are the customer's instructions. If Glideworth believes an instruction breaches Data Protection Law it will tell
the customer. If the law requires Glideworth to process the data otherwise, it will say so first unless the law
forbids it.

## 3. Confidentiality

Glideworth makes sure that people allowed to process the data are bound by confidentiality. Staff cannot see the
contents of a workspace except through a support grant the customer creates (Annex II).

## 4. Security

Glideworth takes the measures in Annex II, which are meant to meet Article 32 of the GDPR. Glideworth may change
them if the protection is not reduced.

## 5. Sub-processors

The customer gives general authorisation for Glideworth to use the sub-processors on the
[sub-processor list](/legal/subprocessors/). Glideworth will:

- keep that list up to date and give the customer at least 30 days' notice, by email to account holders and on
  that list, of a new or replacement sub-processor, so the customer can object on reasonable data protection
  grounds;
- if the customer objects and the parties cannot agree, allow the customer to stop using the service for the
  affected data and receive a refund of prepaid fees for the unused period;
- put terms on each sub-processor that give the same level of protection as this agreement; and
- remain responsible to the customer for each sub-processor's performance.

## 6. International transfers

Glideworth stores customer data in the European Union (servers in the Netherlands, backups in Germany). Some
sub-processors handle part of the data outside the EU, mainly in the United States; the [sub-processor
list](/legal/subprocessors/) gives the location and the transfer safeguard for each. Glideworth is based in the
United Arab Emirates, and its staff may reach that data from there, only for support and operations and only under the access
controls in Annex II. The EU has not made an adequacy decision for the UAE, and Glideworth does not rely on an
adequacy decision from the UK for it.

To the extent the customer's use of the service is a transfer that needs a safeguard under Chapter V of the
GDPR or the UK GDPR, the parties agree the following, and they apply only to that extent:

- The Commission has said that those clauses cover transfers to importers who are not subject to the GDPR. If

## 7. Helping the customer

Taking account of the nature of the processing, Glideworth will:

- help the customer answer data subject requests. The customer can use the in-app export and deletion tools
  and the personal-data download directly. Glideworth will pass on any request it receives about the customer's
  data and will not answer it itself except to say it was passed on;
- help the customer meet its obligations about security, breach notification, data protection impact
  assessments and prior consultation, so far as it relates to the processing and the information Glideworth has.

## 8. Personal data breaches

Glideworth will tell the customer without undue delay, and aims to do so within 48 hours, after becoming aware
of a personal data breach affecting the customer's data, with the information it then has: what happened, the
data and people affected, likely consequences, and what has been done. It will give further information as it
learns it.

## 9. Deletion and return

At the end of the service, or when the customer asks, the customer can export a complete copy and delete the
workspace in the app. On deletion the live database is deleted at once and the workspace's backup key is
destroyed, so its encrypted backups can no longer be read; the backup files are then removed from storage. Backups
of the account database, which hold account records but no workspace contents, age out within a further 35 days. If the customer asks Glideworth to delete it, Glideworth does so within 30 days. Glideworth
keeps only what the law requires, such as billing and accounting records, and a minimal record that the
workspace existed. A workspace that is read-only after a lapse is deleted on the timetable in the
[privacy policy](/legal/privacy/), section 7, with warning emails 30 and 7 days before, so the customer should
export what it needs before then.

## 10. Information and audits

Glideworth will give the customer the information needed to show compliance with Article 28, including a
reasonable written description of its security measures, and will allow and contribute to audits (including
inspections) by the customer or an auditor it appoints, on reasonable notice, no more than once a year unless
there has been a breach, during working hours, subject to confidentiality and without exposing other customers'
data. The customer pays its own costs, and Glideworth's reasonable costs beyond a first request.
Glideworth holds **no security certification** at the date of this document and does not claim any.

## 11. Liability and order of precedence

Liability under this agreement is subject to the limits in the terms, to the extent the law allows. Nothing in
this agreement or the terms limits the rights of data subjects, or any liability to them that the law does not
allow to be limited. If this agreement conflicts with the terms on the processing of personal data, this
agreement prevails.

## Annex II: Security measures

These are the measures in place on the hosted service.

- **In transit and at rest.** TLS for all traffic. The data volume is encrypted (LUKS). Backups encrypted per workspace
  with their own key (AES-256-GCM). The service is **not end-to-end encrypted**: Glideworth's servers can read the
  workspace.
- **Separation.** Each workspace is a separate database file. Credentials carry the workspace identity, and
  tests check that one workspace cannot read another.
- **Authentication.** Passwords stored as salted scrypt hashes. Tokens, API keys and recovery codes stored as
  hashes. Optional two-factor sign-in with an authenticator app, with the secret stored encrypted. Sign-in
  throttling and bot checks. Password re-entry for sensitive
  actions. Session lists and revocation. New-device alerts.
- **Access.** Operator console reachable only through an encrypted tunnel to the server, never from the public
  internet, with mandatory two-factor sign-in. It does not show
  workspace contents. Time-limited, customer-created, read-only support grants are the only route to contents,
  and each view is logged in the workspace's audit trail. The customer is emailed when a grant is first used. This applies to staff wherever they are, including in
  the United Arab Emirates.
- **Least data in logs.** An allowed-field logging rule. No financial values, request bodies, query strings,
  cookies, tokens or error text in logs or error reports, enforced by an automated test.
- **Backups and recovery.** Changed workspaces are backed up every 15 minutes and the account database every 5
  minutes, encrypted before upload. Every backup is kept for 48 hours, then the newest each day for 30 days. A
  restore of sample workspaces is tested every day. Targets of 15 minutes for data loss
  and 15 minutes to restore one workspace are objectives, not guarantees.
- **Deletion.** Workspace deletion removes the live database and destroys the workspace's backup key, then
  removes its backups (section 9).
- **Audit trail.** Account security events and per-workspace data changes are recorded.
- **Network and host.** Firewall, hardened host, TLS with strict transport security, security headers.
- **Incident handling.** A written incident process and a security contact (<a
  href={`mailto:${site.contact.security}`}>{site.contact.security}</a>).
- **Not done.** Application-level encryption of live databases. Third-party security certifications.
