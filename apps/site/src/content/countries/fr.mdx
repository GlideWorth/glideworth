France is estimated at a **basic level**: the main rules are in, with {facts.simplificationCount('fr')} written
simplifications, and it has had less checking than the United Kingdom or the United States. Income tax uses the
barème from the 2026 finance law (loi de finances du 19 février 2026), which taxes 2025 income and is the latest
enacted. The figures were checked against service-public.gouv.fr and BOFiP on 19 November 2026.

The barème for 2026 income will be set by the 2027 finance law, which was still before Parliament on that date. It is
**not applied**: the model carries the 2025-income barème forward with the plan's threshold policy. That includes a
proposed €3,000 sub-cap on the 10% pension allowance. Social contributions use 2026 rates.

## What is modelled

- **Barème**: 0% to €11,600; 11% to €29,579; 30% to €84,577; 41% to €181,917; 45% above, per part of the quotient
  familial. The benefit of the quotient familial is capped at €1,807 per half-part, and at €4,262 for the two
  half-parts of a single parent's first child. The décote reduces the tax of lower-income households (maximum €897
  for a single person, €1,483 for a couple, at 45.25%).
- **Salaries** are gross. Employee pension contributions (vieillesse capped at the €48,060 social security ceiling
  at 6.9%, the uncapped 0.4%, and AGIRC-ARRCO) and CSG at 9.2% (6.8 points deductible) and CRDS at 0.5% on 98.25% of salary
  are deducted. A 10% deduction for professional expenses applies (minimum €509, maximum €14,555 per person).
- **Pensions** pay CSG, CRDS and CASA at the rate for the household's income: nothing up to €13,048 of income for one
  part; 3.8% CSG plus 0.5% CRDS up to €17,057; 6.6% plus 0.5% plus 0.3% CASA up to €26,472; and 8.3% plus 0.5% plus 0.3%
  above that. Each extra half-part raises the limits. The law uses the household's income of two years earlier. The
  model uses the same year's income. Pensions get a 10% income-tax allowance (minimum €454 a person, maximum €4,439 a
  household). PER withdrawals are taxed as pensions.
- **Capital income**: the PFU is 31.4% (12.8% income tax plus 18.6% social contributions) on income received from
  2026. Assurance-vie and property income keep 17.2% social contributions. Dividends get a 40% allowance under the
  barème option.
- **Wrappers**: PEA, assurance-vie and PER. Gains are taxed on withdrawal and assumed held five years (PEA) or eight
  years (assurance-vie), both settings. After five years PEA gains are free of income tax but pay the 18.6% social
  contributions. After eight years assurance-vie has a €4,600 allowance (€9,200 for a couple) and a 7.5% rate. The PER
  deduction is capped at 10% of professional income.
- **Legal retirement age** follows the 2023 reform as suspended by the 2026 social security financing law: 62 for
  people born before September 1961, 62 years 9 months for those born from 1963 to March 1965, 63 for the rest of
  1965, and three months more for each later year, reaching 64 for people born from 1969. The PER opens at the same
  age. You enter the pension amount yourself.

## Worked example

<Callout type="note" title="Illustrative">
  Made-up figures, to show the arithmetic with the 2026 finance law barème. It assumes a single employee with no
  children, no other income and no deductions beyond those shown. It is not a forecast or advice.
</Callout>

A single employee has a gross salary of €40,000.

- Employee pension contributions: 6.9% × €40,000 = €2,760; 0.4% × €40,000 = €160; AGIRC-ARRCO 4.01% × €40,000 =
  €1,604 (all in the first tranche, as €40,000 is below the ceiling). Total **€4,524**.
- CSG and CRDS: base = 98.25% × €40,000 = €39,300; × (9.2% + 0.5%) = **€3,812.10**, of which the deductible CSG is 6.8% × €39,300 = €2,672.40.
- Net taxable before expenses: €40,000 − €4,524 − €2,672.40 = €32,803.60. Less the 10% deduction (€3,280.36) gives
  €29,523.24.
- Barème: 11% × (€29,523.24 − €11,600 = €17,923.24) = €1,971.56.
- Décote: €897 − 45.25% × €1,971.56 = €4.87, because the tax is below the décote limit.
- Income tax: €1,971.56 − €4.87 = **€1,966.69**.

## Historical data for simulations

No French or euro-area market history is bundled. French households are tested against US history by default

## What to treat with care

- The barème is for 2025 income and is carried forward, which will be wrong when the 2027 finance law changes it.
- The CSG rate on pensions is chosen from the same year's income. The law uses the income of two years earlier and
  smooths a change of rate.
- PEA and assurance-vie dividends are kept inside the wrapper (no annual tax), and part of the gain is taxed on withdrawal.
- The rate of 12.8% above €150,000 of assurance-vie premiums is not modelled, nor are the older rules for premiums paid
  before 27 September 2017.
- The PER deduction is limited to 10% of this year's professional income, while the law uses the prior year and allows
  carry-forward of unused ceilings.
- Early retirement for long careers is not modelled.
- No preset has been checked line by line against an official calculator for every case.
