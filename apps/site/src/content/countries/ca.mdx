Canada has a detailed federal and provincial tax model for 2026, with {facts.simplificationCount('ca-federal')} written
simplifications for the federal part and {facts.simplificationCount('ca-province')} for the provincial part. The pension side is less
complete: the Canada Pension Plan and Old Age Security are modelled, but the project's status notes list CPP, OAS and the
Guaranteed Income Supplement as partial, and GIS is an amount you enter. For what Glideworth does for a Canadian plan
as a whole, see the [Canada retirement planner](/ca/).

Plans use calendar years. Years after 2026 index thresholds with the plan's threshold policy, except where a province
has paused indexation in law (below). The figures were checked against the Canada Revenue Agency and provincial
sources on 19 November 2026.

## Federal tax, 2026

Federal brackets: 14% to $58,523; 20.5% to $117,045; 26% to $181,440; 29% to $258,482; 33% above. Credits are
worked at 14%. The basic personal amount is $16,452, reducing to $14,829 between net incomes of $181,440 and
$258,482. The age amount is $9,208, reduced by 15% of net income above $46,432 (net income after any OAS repayment),
the pension income amount $2,000 and the Canada employment amount $1,501. Eligible dividends are grossed up by 38%
and non-eligible by 15%, with federal credits of 15.0198% and 9.0301% of the grossed-up amount. Capital gains are
50% included: the proposed increase to two-thirds was cancelled in March 2025. Pension income splitting is chosen
in 5% steps to minimise federal and provincial tax together.

## CPP, EI and OAS

The 2026 Canada Pension Plan figures are a maximum pensionable earnings figure of $74,600, an additional maximum of
$85,000 and a basic exemption of $3,500; employees pay 4.95% base plus 1% enhanced, and 4% on the second tier
(CPP2). From 2027 the base rate falls to 4.75% (Bill C-30), so employees pay 5.75% in total. CPP contributions stop
at 70. Quebec uses QPP at 6.3% (5.3% base plus 1% enhanced); QPP's 2027 rate has not been confirmed, so the 2026
rate is kept. EI premiums are 1.63% up to $68,900 of earnings (1.3% in Quebec). Old Age Security is reduced by a
15% recovery tax on net income above $95,323; it is applied in the same year, not with the one-year lag of real
instalments. The default CPP start age is 65.

## Accounts, limits and access

- **RRSP**: deduction limit of 18% of earned income up to $33,810; no contributions after age 71. RRIF minimum
  withdrawals follow CRA's prescribed factors (1 / (90 − age) up to 70; 5.28% at 71, rising to 20% from 95).
- **TFSA**: $7,000 a year from age 18; withdrawals are tax-free.
- **FHSA**: $8,000 a year; withdrawals are treated as tax-free.
- RESP, LIRA and non-registered accounts are available as account types.

CRA has published 2027 figures (an RRSP limit of $35,390 and maximum pensionable earnings of $76,700). Glideworth does
not use them yet: it indexes the 2026 figures.

## Provinces and territories

All ten provinces and three territories have their own brackets, basic amounts, age and pension amounts and
dividend tax credits, taken from CRA's July 2026 payroll formulas (T4127) and the 2026 TD1 forms. Points to know:

- **Ontario**: the surtax (20% of Ontario tax over $5,818 and 36% over $7,446) is worked before the Ontario dividend
  tax credit, as on the ON428 form, and the Health Premium (up to $900) is added.
- **Manitoba**: brackets of $47,000 and $100,000 and a basic personal amount of $15,780, which Manitoba stopped
  indexing from 2025, so they stay at these values in later years. The basic personal amount is reduced to nil
  between net incomes of $200,000 and $400,000.
- **British Columbia**: the first rate is 5.60% for 2026 (Budget 2026). Brackets and credits are not indexed from
  2027 to 2030.
- **Prince Edward Island**: a 20% rate applies above $200,000 from 2026.
- **Newfoundland and Labrador**: the basic personal amount is $13,094 for 2026 and $15,000 from 2027.
- **Saskatchewan**: the senior supplementary amount of $2,569 is added from 65.
- **Yukon**: the basic personal amount follows the federal one, including its reduction for high incomes.
- **Quebec**: the federal abatement of 16.5% and QPP are applied. Quebec's 2026 brackets ($54,345, $108,680 and
  $132,245) and basic personal amount ($18,952) come from Quebec's Ministère des Finances.

## Worked example

<Callout type="note" title="Illustrative">
  Made-up figures, to show the arithmetic with the 2026 federal table. This covers federal income tax only, for
  an employee who has no other deductions. It is not a forecast or advice.
</Callout>

A person earns $60,000 in wages in 2026.

- CPP contributions: ($60,000 − $3,500 exemption) × 4.95% = $2,796.75 base, and × 1% = $565.00 enhanced. The base
  part earns a credit; the enhanced part is deducted from income.
- EI premiums: $60,000 × 1.63% = $978.00.
- Taxable income: $60,000 − $565.00 = $59,435.
- Tax on brackets: 14% × $58,523 = $8,193.22; 20.5% × ($59,435 − $58,523 = $912) = $186.96. Total $8,380.18.
- Credits at 14% on $16,452 (basic personal) + $1,501 (Canada employment) + $2,796.75 (CPP) + $978.00 (EI) =
  $21,727.75, which is $21,727.75 × 14% = $3,041.885.
- Federal income tax: $8,380.18 − $3,041.885 = **$5,338.295**, about $5,338.30. Provincial tax comes on top.

## Historical data for simulations

No Canadian market history is bundled. Canadian households are tested against US history by default: the
S&P 500, 10-year Treasuries, T-bills and CPI-U from 1928 to 2025, in US dollars. Canadian history is on the project's list of data sets to add. Datasets are not updated automatically.

## What to treat with care

- Provincial dividend tax credit rates are CRA's 2025 rates; the 2026 worksheets are not published yet.
- Manitoba's brackets: CRA's payroll formulas and Manitoba Finance give $47,000 and $100,000, but CRA's page of
  2026 rates shows $47,564 and $101,200. Glideworth uses $47,000 and $100,000; the conflict is not resolved.
- Quebec's three lower rates (14%, 19% and 24%) and Quebec's dividend tax credits could not be confirmed, because
  Revenu Québec's site refused automated access. The top rate of 25.75% is confirmed by the Manitoba Budget 2026
  comparison of provincial rates.
- Newfoundland and Labrador's $15,000 basic personal amount from 2027 may be indexed for 2027; that is not
  confirmed.
- The tables are for 2026; later years index them, except for the legislated changes above.
- The Quebec model leaves out several items of the separate Quebec return.
