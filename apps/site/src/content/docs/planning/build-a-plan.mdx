A plan has two layers. **Current finances** is what you own and owe today. The **Plan editor** is what
happens next. Change today's balances in one place and every plan that starts now follows.

**Before you start:** a household, which [the first-plan setup](/docs/getting-started/first-plan/) creates.

## Current finances

Open **Current finances** in the sidebar (under Overview). The page lists accounts, real assets and debts,
and a household sheet with the people ("About you").

<Steps>
1. **Accounts.** Add each account with its type, owner and balance. The type matters: an ISA is tax-free,
   a SIPP or pension is taxed as income when drawn (with a share tax-free where the country allows),
   a general investment account pays capital gains and dividend tax. Cash, investments and pensions
   behave differently in drawdown.
2. **Real assets.** A home, a car or a rental: a current value, an optional loan, and running costs.
   A rental can carry rent and costs, and an asset can be sold at a milestone.
3. **Debts.** A balance, an interest rate and a monthly payment.
4. **Foreign items.** Accounts, property, debts and income each have a **Country** field. When the country
   implies a different currency, the app offers to switch and asks for an exchange rate if it has none.
   See [Tax settings and currency](/docs/planning/tax-and-currency/).
5. **Update balances later** from the **Progress** page with "Update balances", which can record a snapshot.
   See [Progress and check-ins](/docs/planning/progress-and-check-ins/).
</Steps>

## The Plan editor

Open **Plans**, choose a plan, and use the Plan editor. Its sections are, in order: Timeline, Milestones,
Where you live, Income, Expenses, Flows, Accounts, Property &amp; debts, Withdrawals, Assumptions, Taxes,
Chance of success, What-if and Plan details.

### Milestones

Named moments with a date, an age or a condition. Typical ones: "Retirement" at age 60, "Mortgage paid off",
"Life expectancy" (the plan runs to the last life-expectancy milestone, never more than 100 years), and
"Financial independence" (say, when investments reach 25 times your spending). Income, expenses and flows can
start or end at a milestone. A milestone that is never reached is reported.

### Income and expenses

Each item has an amount (value, basis, frequency), an owner, a start and an end. Income covers salary,
pensions (including defined benefit), the State Pension in the UK, Social Security estimates in the US,
rental and other sources. Expenses can be essential or discretionary, and a category can follow its own
inflation rate (healthcare, education, housing or your own).

### Flows and the savings waterfall

Flows decide what happens to money left over each year, **in priority order**: for example top up an
emergency fund to a target, contribute to the workplace pension (with the employer match), fill the ISA,
then the SIPP, then invest the rest. The order of the list is the funding priority. Whatever is
left after the last flow is saved to cash, or counts as spending, as you choose. Contribution limits come
from the account's country.

### Drawdown order

**Withdrawals** sets where money comes from when spending exceeds income. The default order is cash, then
taxable investments, then pensions, then tax-free accounts. You can reorder it and set when each account
can be touched. Pension wrappers that cannot legally be drawn early stay locked until their access age unless
you model an early-withdrawal penalty. Withdrawals are taxed according to the account type. The same section
holds the withdrawal strategy (fixed amount, percentage, guardrails, variable percentage withdrawal and
others) and flexible spending.

### Assumptions

Growth, dividends, inflation, fees, the cash-flow timing setting (`start`, `mid` or `end`) and
exchange rates. Defaults come from your country. Inflation can vary by category.

### Taxes

How tax is calculated. See [Tax settings and currency](/docs/planning/tax-and-currency/).

## What this does not do

- It does not add survivor pensions beyond what you enter. A survivor what-if says so.
- It does not model events inside a year, randomised lifespan, or care costs inside the Monte Carlo
  (a care scenario is a what-if).
- It does not tell you if an input is wrong. The audit under **Insights** catches common mistakes, such as
  counting housing twice, not every one.

<Callout type="tip" title="Save before you experiment">
  Every change to a plan is a new version, so you can always go back. To try an idea without touching your
  main plan, make a branch. See [Branches, compare and what-ifs](/docs/planning/branches-and-what-ifs/).
</Callout>
