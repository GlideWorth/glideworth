The United States is, with the United Kingdom, the most detailed model in Glideworth. Federal and payroll tax
cover 2026 under the law as amended by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA). Every state and DC are modelled,
and five cities. Glideworth carries {facts.simplificationCount('us-federal')} written simplifications for the
federal model alone, and shows them beside your tax results.

Plans use calendar years. Years after 2026 index the 2026 brackets with the plan's threshold policy; a few
thresholds fixed in statute, such as the net investment income tax and Social Security benefit-taxation
thresholds, stay fixed.

## Federal income tax, 2026

| | Single | Married filing jointly |
|---|---|---|
| Brackets | 10% to $12,400; 12% to $50,400; 22% to $105,700; 24% to $201,775; 32% to $256,225; 35% to $640,600; 37% above | 10% to $24,800; 12% to $100,800; 22% to $211,400; 24% to $403,550; 32% to $512,450; 35% to $768,700; 37% above |
| Standard deduction | $16,100 | $32,200 |
| Extra deduction at 65 or over | $2,050 | $1,650 each |
| 0% rate on gains and qualified dividends, up to taxable income of | $49,450 | $98,900 |
| 15% rate up to | $545,500 | $613,700 |

Head of household, married filing separately and qualifying surviving spouse have their own rows. The senior
deduction of $6,000 is available from 2025 to 2028 and phases out at 6% above $75,000 of income ($150,000 joint).
The net investment income tax is 3.8% above $200,000 ($250,000 joint). The child tax credit is $2,200 per child,
$1,700 of it refundable, phasing out above $200,000 ($400,000 joint). Social Security benefit taxation (the 50%
and 85% tiers), IRMAA surcharges and the ACA premium tax credit are modelled too. The enhanced ACA credits
expired after 2025; a setting turns them back on if you want to test that.

## Payroll tax and Social Security

Employee Social Security tax is 6.2% on wages up to $184,500 (2026), Medicare 1.45% on all wages, and the
Additional Medicare Tax 0.9% above $200,000 ($250,000 joint). Self-employment tax is included. Pre-tax 401(k)
deferrals do not reduce these taxes, as the law says.

For Social Security benefits, Glideworth can estimate the benefit from an earnings record (primary insurance
amount, claiming age, the earnings test, spousal and survivor benefits), or you can enter an amount with a
cost-of-living adjustment. Up to 85% of the benefit can be taxable, using the statutory thresholds
($25,000 and $34,000 single, $32,000 and $44,000 joint).

## Accounts, limits and access

Glideworth knows 401(k), Roth 401(k), 403(b), 457(b), 401(a), solo 401(k), TSP, traditional, Roth, SEP, SIMPLE and
inherited IRAs, HSA, 529 and Coverdell accounts, and taxable brokerage accounts. 2026 limits in the tables:

- Elective deferrals to a 401(k) or similar plan: $24,500, plus $8,000 catch-up from age 50, or $11,250 at ages 60
  to 63.
- IRA: $7,500, plus a $1,100 catch-up from age 50.
- SIMPLE: $17,000, plus $4,000 catch-up, or $5,250 at ages 60 to 63. SEP or solo total: $72,000.
- HSA: $4,400 for self-only cover, $8,750 for family cover, plus $1,000 from age 55.

Early withdrawals before 59 and a half carry a 10% penalty unless the account setting says otherwise; 72(t)
payments are penalty-free. Non-qualified HSA withdrawals before 65 carry a 20% additional tax, and non-qualified
529 earnings 10%. Roth earnings are qualified after 59 and a half and five tax years from the first
contribution. Required minimum distributions follow the SECURE 2.0 starting age (73 for people born 1951 to 1959,
75 from 1960) and the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table. Roth conversions and tax-strategy comparison are available.
Main-home sale gains are excluded up to $250,000 ($500,000 joint). The federal estate tax exclusion is $15,000,000.

## State and city tax

All 50 states and DC use their 2026 rates and brackets. Each state's figures were checked on 16 November 2026 against
that state's revenue department, statute or tax-year forms, which the tax results cite. Eight state sites (Idaho,
Kansas, Louisiana, Michigan, New Hampshire, Ohio, Rhode Island and Wisconsin) could not be opened, and some figures
of nine other states could not be confirmed; those states' results say which figures are not confirmed from an
official source. Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas and Wyoming levy no tax on wages, and
Washington taxes only long-term capital gains. Every state starts from federal adjusted gross income. California's
state disability insurance (1.3% of wages) is included in the California row. City income tax is modelled for New
York City, Philadelphia, Wilmington (Delaware), Detroit and Newark (which levies nothing on residents); the
Wilmington and Newark pages could not be opened. Twenty-four state rows carry a noted approximation, for example for a married-couple threshold or a pension
exclusion; check them before relying on a figure for a particular return.

## Worked example

<Callout type="note" title="Illustrative">
  Made-up figures, to show the arithmetic with the 2026 tables. This is not a forecast or advice.
</Callout>

A single person earns $90,000 in wages in 2026, with no pre-tax deferrals and no itemised deductions.

- Taxable income: $90,000 − $16,100 standard deduction = $73,900.
- Federal income tax: 10% × $12,400 = $1,240; 12% × ($50,400 − $12,400 = $38,000) = $4,560; 22% × ($73,900 −
  $50,400 = $23,500) = $5,170. Total **$10,970**.
- Payroll tax: Social Security 6.2% × $90,000 = $5,580; Medicare 1.45% × $90,000 = $1,305. Total **$6,885**.
- Federal and payroll tax together: $10,970 + $6,885 = **$17,855**. State and city tax would come on top.

## Historical data for simulations

Glideworth's default for US households is US market history from 1928 to 2025: the S&P 500, 10-year Treasury
bonds, Treasury bills and CPI-U, in dollars (from Damodaran's published series; BLS CPI is public domain). The
longer series from 1871 (Shiller's data) is no longer bundled because it has no explicit licence; self-hosters can
download it for their own use. Datasets are not updated automatically, and crypto has no history, so it is sampled from a
distribution.

## What to treat with care

- The "TCJA sunset" setting, which models the 2017 tax law returning, is an estimate: the 2017 brackets and
  deductions multiplied by 1.33, with personal exemptions restored. It is not law.
- Itemised deductions are what you enter. The SALT cap is not applied for you.
- The AMT adds back only the standard deduction, and the QBI deduction treats self-employment income as a
  specified service business.
- IRMAA is estimated from income two years earlier (or the latest year early in the plan), using the 2026 tiers
  indexed like other thresholds.
- Residence is the household's state for the whole year. A relocation milestone switches state from the next
  period.
- Dollar thresholds in later years are projected, so a figure for 2035 is a projection, not a published rate.
