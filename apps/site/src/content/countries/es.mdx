Spain is estimated at a **basic level**: the main rules are in, with {facts.simplificationCount('es')} written
simplifications, and it has had less checking than the United Kingdom or the United States. The income tax scales are for
**2025**, the latest the tax agency has published; the 2026 regional scales were not yet consolidated. They are carried
forward with the plan's threshold policy. Social security uses 2026 rates. The model covers the territorio común and
not Navarra or the País Vasco.

## What is modelled

- **State general scale (2025)**: 9.5% to €12,450; 12% to €20,200; 15% to €35,200; 18.5% to €60,000; 22.5% to €300,000;
  24.5% above. Each region applies its own scale to the other half of the tax. Madrid, Cataluña, Andalucía and
  Comunitat Valenciana have their own scales; every other region uses a scale equal to the state one.
- **Savings scale**: each half runs 9.5% to €6,000; 10.5% to €50,000; 11.5% to €200,000; 13.5% to €300,000; 15% above, so the
  combined scale runs from 19% to 30%.
- **Minimums**: personal €5,550, with €1,150 more from 65 and €1,400 more from 75; descendants €2,400, €2,700,
  €4,000 and €4,500, and €2,800 more for a child under three.
- **Work income**: €2,000 of other expenses and a reduction for low earners (up to €19,747.50 of net income).
- **Social security (employee)**: 6.5% (4.70% contingencias comunes, 1.55% unemployment, 0.10% training and 0.15% MEI) up to a
  maximum base of €5,101.20 a month.
- **Pension plans** (planes de pensiones): a personal limit of €1,500, with up to €8,500 more through an employer. Withdrawals
  are taxed as work income. The default state pension age is 67; you enter the pension amount yourself.
- **Wealth tax** (off by default): above €700,000 of net worth per person, on the state scale, with Valencia's own scale
  and a 100% bonus for Madrid and Andalucía.

## Worked example

<Callout type="note" title="Illustrative">
  Made-up figures, to show the arithmetic with the 2025 scale and 2026 social security, for a person in a region with
  no scale of its own. It is not a forecast or advice.
</Callout>

An employee has a gross salary of €30,000.

- Social security: 6.5% × €30,000 = **€1,950**.
- Net work income: €30,000 − €1,950 − €2,000 other expenses = **€26,050**. (This is above the €19,747.50 limit, so no
  low-earner reduction applies.)
- State scale on €26,050: 9.5% × €12,450 = €1,182.75; 12% × €7,750 = €930.00; 15% × (€26,050 − €20,200 = €5,850) =
  €877.50. Total €2,990.25.
- Less the scale applied to the personal minimum: 9.5% × €5,550 = €527.25. State half of the tax: **€2,463.00**.
- The regional half uses the same scale in a region with no scale of its own: another **€2,463.00**.
- Income tax: €2,463.00 + €2,463.00 = **€4,926.00**.

## Historical data for simulations

No Spanish or euro-area market history is bundled. Spanish households are tested against US history by default
(S&P 500, 10-year Treasuries, T-bills and CPI-U, 1928 to 2025, in US dollars).

## What to treat with care

- The 2025 scales are carried forward into later years, and only four regions have their own.
- Only individual returns are modelled, and regional minimums and deductions are not.
- Descendant minimums are split evenly between two parents and count dependants under 25.
- The wealth tax uses period-start net worth and ignores the main-home exemption.
- No preset has been checked line by line against an official calculator for every case.
