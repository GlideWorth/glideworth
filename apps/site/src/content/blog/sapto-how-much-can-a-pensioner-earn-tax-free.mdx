In 2026-27 a single age pensioner in Australia with no other offsets pays no income tax on taxable income up to
about **A$36,960**, and no Medicare levy up to **A$44,268**. The reason is the seniors and pensioners tax offset
(SAPTO): a non-refundable offset of up to A$2,230 for a single person, which sits on top of the A$18,200 tax-free
threshold and the low income tax offset. For each member of a couple the tax-free point is about A$32,770. The
tax-free threshold for pensioners is not a separate threshold in law; it is the result of these offsets together.

This article explains who qualifies for SAPTO, the 2026-27 SAPTO thresholds (and the 2025-26 ones still used in
this season's tax returns), how the 12.5-cent taper works, why income from super after 60 does not count, and
what the numbers look like for a single age pensioner.

## What is SAPTO?

The seniors and pensioners tax offset is an amount the ATO takes off the income tax you owe when it assesses your
return. It is non-refundable: it can reduce your tax to nil, but any unused part is not paid to you (a couple can
transfer an unused amount between them, covered below). You do not apply for it separately; the ATO works it out
from your return ([ATO, Seniors and pensioners tax offset](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/income-deductions-offsets-and-records/tax-offsets/seniors-and-pensioners-tax-offset)).

Being entitled to SAPTO also gives you a higher Medicare levy low-income threshold. You must be entitled to at
least $1 of SAPTO to use it.

## Who is eligible for SAPTO?

You need to meet two conditions for the income year: a pension condition and an income condition.

**The pension condition.** You meet it if, during the year, you received one of the listed Australian Government
payments: the Age Pension, Carer Payment, Disability Support Pension (if you are age-pension age), Education Entry
Payment, Parenting Payment (single), or a listed Department of Veterans' Affairs pension or payment. You also meet
it if you were age-pension age and eligible for the Age Pension but did not receive it, because you did not claim
or because the income or assets test stopped it, and you meet one of the residence conditions, the most common
being 10 years of Australian residence for age-pension purposes, with at least 5 of them continuous. The
age-pension age is 67 for 2023-24 and later years. There is a similar route for veterans
([ATO, Seniors and pensioners tax offset](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/income-deductions-offsets-and-records/tax-offsets/seniors-and-pensioners-tax-offset), "Eligibility for an Australian Government pension or allowance").

So self-funded retirees can get SAPTO too, provided they are 67 or over, would qualify for the Age Pension on age
and residence, and their income is under the limits. The ATO's myTax heading says as much: "Seniors and
pensioners (includes self-funded retirees)".

**The income condition.** Your rebate income must be below the cut-out threshold for your situation. If you have a
spouse, the test uses your combined rebate income: in 2025-26, less than A$87,620 for a couple living together, or
less than A$100,104 if you had to live apart because of illness or because one of you was in a nursing home. A
single person's rebate income must be less than A$52,759 in 2025-26
([ATO, Seniors and pensioners tax offset](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/income-deductions-offsets-and-records/tax-offsets/seniors-and-pensioners-tax-offset), "Income limits for 2025–26").
The combined test only decides eligibility; the amount you get is worked out from your own rebate income.

## What counts as rebate income

Rebate income is not the same as taxable income. The ATO's worksheet adds these together
([ATO, myTax 2026 Seniors and pensioners](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/your-tax-return/instructions-to-complete-your-tax-return/mytax-instructions/2026/tax-offsets/seniors-and-pensioners-tax-offset), "Worksheet – Working out rebate income"):

- your taxable income, leaving out any assessable first home super saver released amount
- reportable employer super contributions
- deductible personal super contributions
- net financial investment loss
- net rental property loss
- reportable fringe benefits (from employers exempt from FBT under section 57A, multiplied by 0.53).

The Age Pension is part of your taxable income: the ATO lists it first among taxable government payments
([ATO, Government payments and allowances](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/income-deductions-offsets-and-records/income-you-must-declare/government-payments-and-allowances)).
Interest, dividends with their franking credits, rent and part-time wages count too.

**Super income after 60 does not.** A super income stream paid from a taxed fund to someone aged 60 or over is
"not assessable, not exempt income" ([ATO, Super income stream tax tables](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes/key-superannuation-rates-and-thresholds/super-income-stream-tax-tables), Table 15).
It is not in your taxable income, and it is not one of the amounts the rebate income worksheet adds back, so an
account-based pension from a taxed fund does not reduce your SAPTO. Untaxed elements are different: from 60 they
are taxed at marginal rates with a 10% offset (Table 16), so they are in taxable income and in rebate income.

## SAPTO rates and thresholds for 2026-27

The maximum offset did not change in 2026-27, but the thresholds did, because the first income tax rate fell from
16% to 15% on 1 July 2026
([ATO, Personal income tax: new tax cuts for every Australian taxpayer](https://www.ato.gov.au/about-ato/new-legislation/in-detail/individuals/personal-income-tax-new-tax-cuts-for-every-australian-taxpayer), "SAPTO rates and rebate income thresholds for 2026-27"):

| Status (2026-27) | Maximum offset | Shading-out threshold | Cut-out threshold |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Single | A$2,230 | A$36,034 | A$53,874 |
| Each partner of a couple | A$1,602 | A$31,847 | A$44,663 |
| Each partner of a couple separated by illness | A$2,040 | A$34,767 | A$51,087 |

For the 2025-26 return you lodge now, the figures are
([ATO, Seniors and pensioners tax offset](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/income-deductions-offsets-and-records/tax-offsets/seniors-and-pensioners-tax-offset), "SAPTO rates and rebate income thresholds for 2025–26"):

| Status (2025-26) | Maximum offset | Shading-out threshold | Cut-out threshold |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Single | A$2,230 | A$34,919 | A$52,759 |
| Each partner of a couple | A$1,602 | A$30,994 | A$43,810 |
| Each partner of a couple separated by illness | A$2,040 | A$33,732 | A$50,052 |

If more than one row applies to you during the year, the ATO uses the one that gives the larger offset.

## How the SAPTO taper works

Below the shading-out threshold you get the full offset. Above it, the offset falls by 12.5 cents for every dollar
of rebate income over the threshold, and the result is rounded up to the nearest whole dollar
([ATO, Seniors and pensioners tax offset](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/income-deductions-offsets-and-records/tax-offsets/seniors-and-pensioners-tax-offset), "Tax offset reduction").
The cut-out threshold is where it reaches nil: for a single person in 2026-27, A$36,034 + A$2,230 ÷ 0.125 =
A$53,874.

**Couples.** If both of you are eligible and one of you cannot use all of their offset, the unused part can be
transferred to the other. The ATO does this for you when you both lodge. If your spouse's taxable income is
A$6,000 or less, their whole offset is available to transfer; above that it is reduced by 15 cents for each dollar
over A$6,000. A transfer also raises the receiving partner's own shading-out threshold, using a longer formula the
ATO sets out step by step ("Transferring unused SAPTO between spouses"). The tax-free points in this article are
before any transfer.

## How much can a pensioner earn before paying tax?

Three things combine for a resident aged 67 or over in 2026-27:

- **The tax-free threshold.** Nil tax on the first A$18,200, then 15 cents for each dollar to A$45,000
  ([ATO, Tax rates – Australian resident](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes/tax-rates-australian-residents), "Resident tax rates 2026–27").
- **The low income tax offset.** A$700 for taxable income up to A$37,500, then A$700 minus 5 cents per dollar above
  A$37,500 up to A$45,000, then A$325 minus 1.5 cents per dollar above A$45,000 until it reaches nil at A$66,667
  ([ATO, Low income tax offset](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/income-deductions-offsets-and-records/tax-offsets/low-income-tax-offset)).
- **SAPTO**, from the table above.

Income tax stays at nil while the tax on your income is no more than LITO plus SAPTO. Solving for the point where
they meet gives, before the Medicare levy:

| 2026-27, taxable income equal to rebate income | No income tax up to about |
| --- | ---: |
| Single | A$36,960 |
| Each partner of a couple (no transfer) | A$32,770 |
| Each partner of a couple separated by illness (no transfer) | A$35,690 |

These are our own calculations from the ATO's published rates, not ATO figures, and they assume no other offsets,
deductions or rebate-income add-backs.

**The Medicare levy** has its own, higher threshold for people entitled to SAPTO. For a single person it is not
payable at all on taxable income up to A$44,268, and is reduced up to A$55,335; for everyone else the figures are
A$28,011 and A$35,013
([ATO, Medicare levy reduction for low-income earners](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/medicare-and-private-health-insurance/medicare-levy/medicare-levy-reduction/medicare-levy-reduction-for-low-income-earners), "Medicare levy thresholds for a single individual").
The ATO page is for 2025-26; the Act that set these amounts applies them to 2025-26 and later years
([Treasury Laws Amendment (Delivering an Efficient and Trusted Tax System) Act 2026](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2026A00058/asmade/2026-06-30/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html), Schedule 5, items 2, 4 and 14).
Families have their own thresholds, which this article does not cover.

## A worked example for a single age pensioner

<Callout type="note" title="Illustrative">
  Made-up round figures for 2026-27, for a single resident aged 70 who receives the Age Pension. It is not a
  forecast or advice, and the Age Pension amount is not the actual rate.
</Callout>

Margaret receives A$30,000 of Age Pension and A$6,000 of bank interest. She also draws A$20,000 from an
account-based pension in a taxed super fund. She has no deductions, no losses and no fringe benefits.

1. **Taxable income:** A$30,000 + A$6,000 = **A$36,000**. The A$20,000 from super is not assessable, so it is left
   out.
2. **Rebate income:** the same A$36,000, which is below the A$36,034 shading-out threshold, so she gets the full
   SAPTO of A$2,230.
3. **Tax on taxable income:** 15% × (A$36,000 − A$18,200 = A$17,800) = A$2,670.
4. **Offsets:** LITO A$700 + SAPTO A$2,230 = A$2,930, which is more than A$2,670. Income tax is **nil**. The
   remaining A$260 is lost, as SAPTO is non-refundable and she has no spouse to transfer it to.
5. **Medicare levy:** taxable income of A$36,000 is below A$44,268, so **nil**.

She spends A$56,000 a year and pays no income tax or Medicare levy.

**If her interest were A$10,000 instead,** taxable and rebate income would be A$40,000:

- Tax: 15% × (A$40,000 − A$18,200 = A$21,800) = A$3,270.
- LITO: A$700 − 5% × (A$40,000 − A$37,500 = A$2,500) = A$575.
- SAPTO: A$2,230 − 12.5% × (A$40,000 − A$36,034 = A$3,966) = A$2,230 − A$495.75 = A$1,734.25, rounded up to
  A$1,735.
- Income tax: A$3,270 − A$575 − A$1,735 = **A$960**.
- Medicare levy: A$40,000 is still below A$44,268, so nil.

The extra A$4,000 of interest costs A$960 in tax, an effective rate of 24% on that slice: A$600 of income tax at
15%, A$125 of lost LITO and A$495 of lost SAPTO, less the A$260 of offset that went unused before. That is the
"shading-out" zone, and it is worth knowing about when you choose between drawing from super, which is tax-free and
not rebate income, and drawing from savings that produce taxable interest.

## Planning with this in Glideworth

Glideworth does not model SAPTO; this is one of its listed simplifications for Australia, so for an eligible
pensioner on a modest income its income tax estimate will be higher than the tax actually payable, by up to the
SAPTO amount. It does apply the seniors and pensioners Medicare levy threshold (A$44,268) from age 67, without
checking SAPTO's other conditions. It does not default an Age Pension for Australian households: if you receive
one, enter it as income, and it is taxed with your other income.

Super in pension phase is modelled as tax-free from 60, and the low income tax offset, franking credits and the
50% capital gains discount are included. The full list of what the Australian model covers and leaves out is on
the [Australia planning page](/planning/au/), and [tax estimates](/features/tax-estimates/) explains how the
estimates are shown.
