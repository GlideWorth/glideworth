New Zealand is estimated at a **basic level**: the main rules are in, with {facts.simplificationCount('nz')} written
simplifications, and it has had less checking than the United Kingdom or the United States. The plan year defaults to the
tax year from 1 April to 31 March, and the table is for 2026-27.

## What is modelled

- **Income tax**: 10.5% to NZ$15,600; 17.5% to NZ$53,500; 30% to NZ$78,100; 33% to NZ$180,000; 39% above.
- **ACC earners' levy**: 1.75% from 1 April 2026, on earnings up to NZ$156,641.
- **Dividends** are assumed fully imputed at the 28% company rate: the dividend is grossed up and the credit
  deducted. Interest is taxed at the marginal rate.
- **No general capital gains tax**, so gains are not taxed.
- **KiwiSaver**: contributions come from taxed pay with no deduction, fund earnings are taxed inside the fund, and
  withdrawals from age 65 are tax-free.
- **NZ Superannuation**: the default age is 65, and you enter the amount yourself.

## Worked example

<Callout type="note" title="Illustrative">
  Made-up figures, to show the arithmetic with the 2026-27 table. It is not a forecast or advice.
</Callout>

An employee earns NZ$70,000 in the 2026-27 tax year.

- Income tax: 10.5% × NZ$15,600 = NZ$1,638.00; 17.5% × (NZ$53,500 − NZ$15,600 = NZ$37,900) = NZ$6,632.50; 30% ×
  (NZ$70,000 − NZ$53,500 = NZ$16,500) = NZ$4,950.00. Total **NZ$13,220.50**.
- ACC earners' levy: 1.75% × NZ$70,000 = **NZ$1,225.00**.
- Together: NZ$13,220.50 + NZ$1,225.00 = **NZ$14,445.50**.
