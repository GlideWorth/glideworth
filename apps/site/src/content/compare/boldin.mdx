Boldin (the trading name of NewRetirement, Inc.) and Glideworth are both retirement planners. They differ most
in who they are built for and how you use them: Boldin is a hosted, US-focused planner with optional human
advice; Glideworth is an open-source planner you can run yourself, with tax estimates for
{facts.taxCountryCount} countries. Every statement about Boldin below links to the page it came from and the
date we read it.

## Who each suits

**Boldin is the better choice if** you live in the United States and want a hosted planner you can start using
today, with human help available. Its pricing page lists a Boldin Advisors service: a one-off plan check-up with
a CERTIFIED FINANCIAL PLANNER professional for a $3,200 flat fee
([pricing](https://boldin.com/pricing/), read 16 Nov 2026). Glideworth offers no advice or coaching. Boldin's
PlannerPlus plan lists connected accounts, so balances can come from your institutions rather than being typed
in ([pricing](https://boldin.com/pricing/), 16 Nov 2026); Glideworth has no account aggregation, by design, and
takes CSV, OFX, QFX and QIF files instead. Boldin also says it holds SOC 2 Type 1 and Type 2 reports and offers
optional two-factor sign-in
([security](https://www.boldin.com/security), 16 Nov 2026); Glideworth holds no certifications, and its hosted service
offers two-factor sign-in with an authenticator app. And Boldin has a longer history: it renamed itself from
NewRetirement in September 2024
([press release](https://www.boldin.com/retirement/press/newretirement-rebrands-boldin/), 16 Nov 2026), and says
more than 450,000 people have complete plans with it ([about](https://www.boldin.com/about), 16 Nov 2026).
Glideworth is new.

**Glideworth may suit you better if** you plan outside the United States or across countries, because Boldin
says its planner has no features for other countries' tax or retirement rules
([help article](https://help.boldin.com/en/articles/6509299-is-plannerplus-suitable-for-canadians-or-other-non-us-citizens-planning-for-retirement),
16 Nov 2026). It also suits you if you want to self-host and keep your data in a file on your own machine, read
or change the source, or connect your own AI assistant or scripts to your plan through an API and an MCP server.

## Price

Boldin's pricing page lists a free Basic plan, PlannerPlus at $14 a month or $168 a year ("billed annually after
your 14 day free trial", "cancel anytime"), and Boldin Advisors at a $3,200 flat fee
([pricing](https://boldin.com/pricing/), read 16 Nov 2026). It does not state how monthly billing works after the
trial.

Glideworth is free to self-host with every feature. The hosted service, Glideworth Cloud, costs
{formatPrice(pricing.monthly)} a month or {formatPrice(pricing.yearly)} a year, after a {pricing.trialDays}-day
free trial that needs no card; see
[pricing](/pricing/).

A year of the hosted plan is {formatPrice(pricing.yearly)}, against $168 for PlannerPlus. They are not
like-for-like: PlannerPlus includes connected accounts and sits beside a human-advice service, and Glideworth's
hosted plan does not. If you self-host, you pay nothing for the software but look after backups and updates
yourself.

## Moving over from Boldin

Glideworth has no Boldin importer, so a move means entering your plan: accounts and balances, income, spending
and milestones. You can bring balances in from a bank or budgeting tool by CSV (Monarch, YNAB or a generic
layout) or from OFX, QFX or QIF files, and create the plan through the web app, the API or an AI assistant over
MCP. Expect Boldin and Glideworth to give different results for the same inputs: they use different tax tables,
different return models and different conventions. Run your plan in both for a while before you rely on either.

If you hold accounts in more than one country, Glideworth lets you set a country on each account, property,
debt and income and shows tax by country; Boldin says its planner has no features for other countries' tax or
retirement rules.
