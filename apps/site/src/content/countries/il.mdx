Israel is estimated at a **basic level**: the main rules are in, with {facts.simplificationCount('il')} written
simplifications, and it has had less checking than the United Kingdom or the United States. The tables are for 2026.
They were checked on 19 November 2026 against the Knesset's Book of Laws, the Knesset Research and Information
Center and the National Insurance Institute. The Tax Authority's own site could not be read automatically. The law
freezes the tax brackets, credit point and surtax threshold for 2025 to 2027, so 2027 uses the 2026 amounts. From 2028
they follow the plan's threshold policy.

## What is modelled

- **Brackets (2026)**, annual, after the Economic Efficiency Law 2026: 10% to ₪84,120; 14% to ₪120,720; 20% to
  ₪228,000; 31% to ₪301,200; 35% to ₪560,280; 47% above.
- **Surtax** (mas yesef): 3% on taxable income above ₪721,560. Since 2025 there is a further 2% on income from capital
  (interest, dividends and gains) above ₪721,560, measured on capital income alone.
- **Credit points**: each is worth ₪2,904 a year (₪242 a month), and a resident gets 2.25, which is ₪6,534.
- **National Insurance and health tax (employee)**: 4.27% (1.04% plus 3.23%) up to ₪7,703 a month (₪92,436 a year),
  and 12.17% (7% plus 5.17%) from there to ₪51,910 a month (₪622,920 a year), until age 67. Glideworth assumes the
  old-age allowance is claimed at 67; an employee receiving it pays no employee share.
- **Capital income**: 25% on interest, dividends and gains.
- **Pensions**: a 35% credit on the employee contribution up to 7% of salary and ₪658 a month, so at most ₪230.30 a
  month. Keren hishtalmut is modelled as a tax-free account; the six-year holding rule is not checked. Pension funds
  open at 67.
- **Old-age allowance** (Bituach Leumi): the default age is 67, the retirement age for men; you enter the amount
  yourself.

## Worked example

<Callout type="note" title="Illustrative">

  credit points and no pension contribution. It is not a forecast or advice.

An employee earns ₪200,000 in 2026.

- Income tax before credits: 10% × ₪84,120 = ₪8,412; 14% × (₪120,720 − ₪84,120 = ₪36,600) = ₪5,124; 20% × (₪200,000 −
  ₪120,720 = ₪79,280) = ₪15,856. Total **₪29,392**.
- Credit points: 2.25 × ₪2,904 = **₪6,534**. Income tax after credits: ₪29,392 − ₪6,534 = **₪22,858**.
- National Insurance and health tax: 4.27% × ₪92,436 = ₪3,947.02; 12.17% × (₪200,000 − ₪92,436 = ₪107,564) = ₪13,090.54.
  Total **₪17,037.56**.

## Historical data for simulations

No Israeli market history is bundled. Israeli households are tested against US history by default (S&P 500, 10-year
Treasuries, T-bills and CPI-U, 1928 to 2025, in US dollars).

## Figures that could not be confirmed

- **The 25% rates** on capital gains, dividends and interest could not be confirmed from an official text.
- Pensions are taxed in full because the partial exemption for qualifying pension annuities could not be confirmed
  from an official source. How the old-age allowance is taxed was not confirmed either; it is taxed as ordinary income.
- Capital gains are taxed on the nominal gain, because Glideworth does not know purchase dates. Long-held assets can
  be over-taxed, since Israel taxes only the real, inflation-adjusted gain.
- No preset has been checked line by line against an official calculator for every case.
