Israel is estimated at a **basic level**: the main rules are in, with {facts.simplificationCount('il')} written
simplifications, and it has had less checking than the United Kingdom or the United States. The tables are for 2026, and
later years index them with the plan's threshold policy.

## What is modelled

- **Brackets (2026)**, annual, after the Economic Efficiency Law 2026: 10% to ₪84,120; 14% to ₪120,720; 20% to
  ₪228,000; 31% to ₪301,200; 35% to ₪560,280; 47% above. A surtax of 3% applies above ₪721,560, with 2% more on
  capital income.
- **Credit points**: each is worth ₪2,904 a year, and a resident gets 2.25, which is ₪6,534.
- **National Insurance and health tax (employee)**: 4.27% (1.04% plus 3.23%) up to ₪92,436 a year, and 12.17% (7% plus 5.17%)
  from there to ₪622,920, until age 67.
- **Capital income**: 25% on interest, dividends and real gains.
- **Pensions**: a 35% credit on the employee contribution up to 7% of salary. Keren hishtalmut is modelled as a
  tax-free account; the six-year holding rule is not checked. Pension funds open at 67 and the default old-age pension age is 67; you enter
  the amount yourself.

## Worked example

  credit points and no pension contribution. It is not a forecast or advice.

An employee earns ₪200,000 in 2026.

- Income tax before credits: 10% × ₪84,120 = ₪8,412; 14% × (₪120,720 − ₪84,120 = ₪36,600) = ₪5,124; 20% × (₪200,000 −
  ₪120,720 = ₪79,280) = ₪15,856. Total **₪29,392**.
- Credit points: 2.25 × ₪2,904 = **₪6,534**. Income tax after credits: ₪29,392 − ₪6,534 = **₪22,858**.
- National Insurance and health tax: 4.27% × ₪92,436 = ₪3,947.02; 12.17% × (₪200,000 − ₪92,436 = ₪107,564) = ₪13,090.54.
  Total **₪17,037.56**.

## Historical data for simulations

No Israeli market history is bundled. Israeli households are tested against US history by default (S&P 500, 10-year
Treasuries, T-bills and CPI-U, 1928 to 2025, in US dollars).

## Figures that could not be confirmed

- **Not confirmed from an official source:** the brackets, credit-point value, capital-income rates, surtax threshold
  and National Insurance rates. The Israel Tax Authority and National Insurance Institute sites refused automated
  access again on 16 November 2026, so the figures could not be read from them; they are as published by
  independent sources, which agree with each other.
- Pensions are taxed in full because the partial exemption for qualifying pension annuities could not be confirmed
  from an official source.
- Capital gains are taxed on the nominal gain, because Glideworth does not know purchase dates. Long-held assets can
  be over-taxed, since Israel taxes only the real, inflation-adjusted gain.
- No preset has been checked line by line against an official calculator for every case.
