Glideworth estimates UK tax for each person in the household, every year of the plan, under independent
taxation: each person is taxed alone and jointly owned income is split equally. The United Kingdom is one of
the two most detailed models Glideworth has, with {facts.simplificationCount('uk')} written simplifications shown beside your tax
results.

Plans can follow calendar years or the April tax year. A calendar-year plan maps year Y onto tax year Y/Y+1;
choose the April alignment for exact UK years. Years after the last table carry the latest rates forward, with
thresholds frozen to April 2031 as legislated and indexed after that, unless you choose another threshold
policy.

## Accounts and wrappers

| Account | What Glideworth does |
|---|---|
| ISA | Overall allowance of £20,000 a year (2026/27). Growth and withdrawals are tax-free. From 6 April 2027 a cash ISA limit of £12,000 applies to savers under 65 inside the overall allowance, as announced in Budget 2025; you can switch it off or change it. |
| Lifetime ISA | £4,000 a year, for people aged 18 to 49; access from 60. |
| SIPP, workplace pension | Contributions relievable up to the higher of your earnings and £3,600 gross, capped by the annual allowance of £60,000 (£10,000 money purchase allowance once you have flexibly accessed a pension). The allowance tapers above £260,000 adjusted income, to a minimum of £10,000. Relief at source, net pay and salary sacrifice are all handled. Tax-free cash is limited by the lump sum allowance of £268,275. |
| General investment account | Taxed on dividends, interest and gains under the rules above. |

The normal minimum pension age is 55, rising to 57 from 6 April 2028. Salary-sacrifice National Insurance relief
is capped at £2,000 a year from April 2029, as announced.

## State Pension

The new State Pension is £241.30 a week in 2026/27 (£230.25 in 2025/26), about £12,548 a year. State Pension age
comes from your birth month: 66 for people born before April 1960, rising month by month to 67 for those born
between April 1960 and March 1961, 67 for April 1961 to March 1977, rising month by month to 68 for April 1977 to
March 1978, and 68 after that.
Deferring past State Pension age adds 1% for every nine weeks deferred. Projections apply a triple-lock setting
to uprating (an earnings premium of 1% and a floor of 2.5%) as plan defaults. National Insurance stops at State
Pension age.

## Worked example

<Callout type="note" title="Illustrative">
  Made-up figures, to show the arithmetic. This is not a forecast or advice.
</Callout>

A person living in England earns £50,000 in 2026/27, with no pension contributions.

- Income tax: taxable income is £50,000 − £12,570 = £37,430, all inside the £37,700 basic-rate band, so tax is
  £37,430 × 20% = **£7,486.00**.
- National Insurance: £50,000 is below the £50,270 upper limit, so everything above £12,570 is charged at 8%:
  £37,430 × 8% = **£2,994.40**.
- Total: £7,486.00 + £2,994.40 = **£10,480.40**, leaving £39,519.60 before any other deductions.

## Historical data for simulations

Monte Carlo and historical runs need market history. Glideworth's UK series covers 1983 to 2025, in sterling: UK
inflation, Bank Rate and 10-year gilts from the Office for National Statistics and the Bank of England, with US shares
converted to sterling, because no openly licensed UK share history exists. It is only 43 years long, all after 1982,
so it has no 1970s inflation and is more optimistic than long UK history. New UK plans therefore default to US history
from 1928 to 2025, in real terms, which is longer; you can choose the UK series instead. Datasets are not updated
automatically.

## What to treat with care

- Tables are for 2025/26 and 2026/27, with the announced changes for 2027/28 and 2029/30. Later years carry rates
  forward, which is wrong whenever the rules change.
- The model applies the pension annual allowance with its taper, but it does not carry forward unused allowance
  from the three previous tax years, which UK law allows. A large contribution can therefore show an allowance
  charge that carry-forward would remove. "Scheme pays" is not modelled either.
- National Insurance is assessed on the year's earnings, not per pay period.
- Student loan interest is ignored, and a write-off applies only when you enter its year.
- The High Income Child Benefit Charge applies only if the plan says Child Benefit is claimed.
- Welsh rates currently equal the rest of the UK. Scottish rates apply to non-savings income; savings,
  dividends and gains use UK rates, as the law does.
- Inheritance tax is an estimate on the Estate page.
- Tax figures for someone who has left the UK, including the personal allowance for non-residents and the frozen
  State Pension, come from the [moving-country model](/planning/moving-country/).
