Ireland is estimated at a **basic level**: the main rules are in, with {facts.simplificationCount('ie')} written
simplifications, and it has had less checking than the United Kingdom or the United States. The tables are for 2026
(Revenue) and 2027 (Budget 2027, announced on 6 October 2026; the Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026 that brings it in was not yet
law in November 2026). Later years carry the 2027 tables forward and index them with the plan's threshold policy.

## What is modelled

| | 2026 | 2027 (Budget 2027) |
|---|---|---|
| Standard rate band, single | €44,000 | €46,500 |
| Single parent | €48,000 | €50,500 |
| Married couple | €53,000, plus up to €35,000 for a second income | €55,500, plus up to €37,500 |
| Personal credit, single / married | €2,000 / €4,000 | €2,125 / €4,250 |
| Employee and earned income credits | €2,000 each, together at most €2,000 | €2,125 each, together at most €2,125 |
| Age credit from 65, single / married | €245 / €490 | €245 / €490 |
| USC | 0.5% to €12,012; 2% to €28,700; 3% to €70,044; 8% above | 2% band to €30,300; otherwise the same |
| Class A employee PRSI | 4.2%, 4.35% from 1 October (average 4.2375%) | 4.35%, 4.5% from 1 October (average 4.3875%) |
| Capital gains tax | 33% to 6 October, 31% from 7 October (average 32.53%) | 31% |

- **Income tax** is 20% up to the rate band and 40% above, less tax credits.
- **USC** is not payable when total income is €13,000 or less. State Pension and other social welfare income are exempt.
- **PRSI**: none at €352 a week or less, and none from age 66. Self-employed income is charged at the same rate. Years
  after 2027 use 4.5%; any later rise is not confirmed.
- **DIRT** at 33% on deposit interest. **Capital gains tax** has a €1,270 annual exemption. Glideworth has no disposal
  dates, so 2026 gains use the average by days.
- **Pension relief** is age-related: 15% of earnings up to age 29, 20% from 30, 25% from 40, 30% from 50, 35% from 55 and 40% from
  60, on earnings capped at €115,000. Pension access is from 60. There is no USC or PRSI relief on contributions.
- **Retirement lump sums**: the tax-free share you set on a pension account is tax-free up to €200,000 over your
  lifetime, taxed at 20% from €200,000 to €500,000 and at 40% above that, where USC is also due.
- **State Pension (Contributory)**: an income you enter, from 66 by default. The maximum personal rate is €299.30 a
  week in 2026 and €309.30 from January 2027. It pays income tax but no USC or PRSI.

## Worked example

<Callout type="note" title="Illustrative">
  Made-up figures, to show the arithmetic with the 2026 table. It assumes a single PAYE employee with no
  pension contributions. It is not a forecast or advice.
</Callout>

A single employee earns €50,000 in 2026.

- Income tax: 20% × €44,000 = €8,800; 40% × (€50,000 − €44,000 = €6,000) = €2,400. Total €11,200. Less credits of
  €2,000 (personal) + €2,000 (employee) = €4,000. Income tax **€7,200**.
- USC: 0.5% × €12,012 = €60.06; 2% × (€28,700 − €12,012 = €16,688) = €333.76; 3% × (€50,000 − €28,700 = €21,300) =
  €639.00. Total **€1,032.82**.
- PRSI: €50,000 × 4.2375% (the 2026 average of 4.2% and 4.35%) = **€2,118.75**.
- Together: €7,200 + €1,032.82 + €2,118.75 = **€10,351.57**.

With the 2027 table the same salary pays income tax of 20% × €46,500 + 40% × €3,500 − €4,250 = €6,450, USC of
€60.06 + 2% × €18,288 + 3% × €19,700 = €1,016.82 and PRSI of 4.3875% × €50,000 = €2,193.75: **€9,660.57** in all.
Both years match the Department of Finance's Budget 2027 table for a single employee on €50,000 (€7,200, €2,119 and
€1,033 before the Budget; €6,450, €2,194 and €1,017 after), which rounds to the euro.

## What to treat with care

- **Funds and ETFs.** Irish and equivalent EU, EEA and OECD funds and ETFs pay exit tax (38% in 2026, 35% from 2027
  as announced), with no annual exemption and a deemed disposal every 8 years. Glideworth does not model exit tax: it
  taxes fund gains at the CGT rate and distributions as income, so the tax on a fund portfolio can be well off. The
  Investment Account announced from 1 July 2027 (1% a year on the value above €50,000, up to €12,000 paid in a year)
  is not modelled either.
- The 2027 figures are Budget 2027 as announced, before the Finance Bill became law.
- Capital gains tax for 2026 is an average of the 33% and 31% rates (32.53%), because the model does not know when
  in the year a gain is made. Development land (33%) and the other special CGT rates are not modelled.
- To model the 25% tax-free retirement lump sum, set a tax-free withdrawal share on the account.
- The tax calculations reproduce Revenue's published 2026 worked examples and the Department of Finance's Budget
  2027 tables for single employees, but no preset has been checked line by line
  against an official calculator for every case.
