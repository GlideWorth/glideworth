The RRIF minimum withdrawal is the fund's value at the start of the year multiplied by a factor for your age at the start of
that year: 1 ÷ (90 − age) up to 70, then a prescribed table from 5.28% at 71 to 20% from 95
([CRA: prescribed factors](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/completing-slips-summaries/t4rsp-t4rif-information-returns/payments/chart-prescribed-factors.html)).
A life income fund (LIF) has the same minimum, plus a **maximum** set by pension law. For an Ontario LIF the 2026 maximum
ranges from 6.45% of the start-of-year balance at 55 to 100% in the year you turn 90, or the previous year's investment
earnings if that is more. Federally regulated LIFs use a different, lower 2026 table.

</Callout>

## RRIF minimum withdrawal: the formula

A registered retirement income fund pays you an income from savings that were in an RRSP. In the year you turn 71 you must
withdraw your RRSP, transfer it to a RRIF or buy an annuity
([CRA: options for your RRSPs](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/rrsps-related-plans/rrsp-options-when-you-turn-71/options-your-rrsps.html)),
and many people open a RRIF earlier.

The rules, from the CRA's guide to RRSPs and other registered plans (T4040) and its page on the minimum amount:

- **Minimum = fair market value of the RRIF at the start of the year × prescribed factor**
  ([CRA: minimum amount from a RRIF](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/completing-slips-summaries/t4rsp-t4rif-information-returns/payments/minimum-amount-a-rrif.html)).
- The factor uses your age in whole years at the beginning of the year.
- No minimum applies in the year the RRIF is set up; it starts the following year
  ([CRA: T4040](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4040/rrsps-other-registered-plans-retirement.html)).
- Under 71, the factor is 1 ÷ (90 − age). From 71 it comes from the table in section 7308 of the
  [Income Tax Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._945/section-7308.html).

### Using your spouse's age

You can choose to base the minimum on your spouse's or common-law partner's age. A younger spouse means a lower factor and a
smaller forced withdrawal. You must choose this on the original RRIF application form, and you cannot change it afterwards
([CRA: T4040](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4040/rrsps-other-registered-plans-retirement.html)).

### Withholding tax on amounts above the minimum

The minimum is not subject to withholding. Anything you take above it has tax withheld at the lump-sum rates
([CRA: RRSP/RRIF FAQ, section 7](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/registered-plans-administrators/registered-retirement-savings-plans-registered-retirement-income-funds-rrsps-rrifs/registered-retirement-savings-plans-registered-retirement-income-funds-rrsps-rrifs.html);
[CRA: tax rates on withdrawals](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/rrsps-related-plans/making-withdrawals/tax-rates-on-withdrawals.html)):

| Amount above the minimum | Outside Quebec | Quebec (federal part) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Up to $5,000 | 10% | 5% |
| Over $5,000, up to $15,000 | 20% | 10% |
| Over $15,000 | 30% | 15% |

In Quebec, provincial tax is withheld as well. Withholding is a prepayment, not the final tax: the whole withdrawal is
income on your return, and you may owe more or get some back. If you split the excess into instalments, the CRA's FAQ
says the rate follows the total for the year, so several small top-ups can attract the higher rate.

<Callout type="note" title="Illustrative">
  Continuing the example: taking $30,000 instead of the $21,600 minimum leaves $8,400 above the minimum. Paid as one
  amount outside Quebec, that falls in the over-$5,000 band, so 20% × $8,400 = $1,680 is withheld. Not a forecast or
  advice.
</Callout>

## What a life income fund is

A LIF holds pension money that is locked in: money from a registered pension plan or a locked-in account (a LIRA or
locked-in RRSP). The CRA describes these as locked-in RRIFs, "sometimes called life income funds or locked-in retirement
income funds"
([CRA: T4040](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4040/rrsps-other-registered-plans-retirement.html)).
Because a LIF is a RRIF for tax purposes, it has the same minimum, and payments are taxed the same way. Pension law adds a
maximum, so the money lasts for life, and limits on taking it out early.

Which rules apply depends on the pension law that governs the money: Ontario's Pension Benefits Act, another province's, or
the federal Pension Benefits Standards Act for federally regulated plans. Each regulator sets its own maximum and unlocking
rules. If you are not sure which applies, your financial institution can tell you.

## LIF maximum withdrawal: the formula

For an Ontario LIF the maximum for a year is the greater of
([FSRA guidance PE0196INF](https://www.fsrao.ca/industry/pensions/regulatory-framework/guidance-pensions/life-income-fund-lif-and-locked-retirement-income-fund-lrif-maximum-annual-income-payment-amount-table)):

1. the investment earnings of the LIF in the previous year, including unrealised gains and losses, and
2. **C ÷ F**, where C is the value at the start of the year and F is the present value of $1 a year, paid in advance, from
   the start of the year to 31 December of the year you turn 90.

F uses the greater of 6% and the long-term Government of Canada bond rate (Statistics Canada CANSIM series V122487) for
November of the applicable year for the first 15 years, and 6% after that. Because that bond rate has been below 6%, Ontario's
percentages do not change from year to year, and FSRA's table applies to any year until a November rate above 6% replaces
it. A third option applies to money transferred in from another LIF in its first year. Percentages are prorated in a first
year of less than 12 months, counting part of a month as a full month.

Federal LIFs use the actual November bond rate for the first 15 years, with no 6% floor, so their maximums change every year.
For 2026 the rate is 3.49%, which gives lower maximums than Ontario's
([OSFI: LIFs, RLIFs and variable benefit accounts](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/pensions/administering-pension-plans/guidance-topic/life-income-funds-restricted-life-income-funds-variable-benefits-accounts)).
If the Income Tax Act minimum is higher than the federal maximum, the minimum is paid.

## LIF vs RRIF

| | RRIF | LIF |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where the money comes from | RRSPs | Locked-in pension money (pension plan, LIRA or locked-in RRSP) |
| Minimum withdrawal | Prescribed factor × start-of-year value | The same |
| Maximum withdrawal | None; tax is withheld above the minimum | Set by pension law (tables above) |
| Taxed as | Income | Income |
| Pension income splitting from 65 | Yes | Yes |
| Taking a lump sum | Any time, taxed as income | Only through unlocking rules |

Both RRIF and LIF payments count as eligible pension income for splitting with a spouse from 65
([CRA: pension income splitting](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/pension-income-splitting.html)).
Both count as income for the OAS recovery tax (see [the OAS clawback in 2026](/blog/oas-clawback-2026/)).

## LIF unlocking

### Ontario: up to 50% within 60 days

Ontario lets you withdraw, or transfer to an RRSP or RRIF, up to 50% of the money transferred into a LIF governed by
Schedule 1.1 to Regulation 909, using FSRA Form 5.2
([FSRA Form 5.2, 2026 edition](https://www.fsrao.ca/sites/default/files/2025-12/PF-106E_26.pdf)). The form sets out the
conditions:

- The financial institution must receive the application no more than 60 days after the money was transferred into the LIF.
- The 50% is of the money transferred in, not of the balance later.
- If you ask for less than 50%, you do not get another chance to take the rest.
- A spouse must consent, unless you have none, live separate and apart, or the money was earned by a former spouse.
- It does not apply to older (Schedule 1) LIFs or LRIFs, or to money moved after 2010 from one Schedule 1.1 LIF to another,
  except under a family law order or agreement.

Money unlocked this way may lose the creditor protection of pension law, and the form warns that any withdrawal or
transfer may have tax consequences.

### Federal: the restricted LIF

Federally regulated locked-in money is unlocked through a restricted life income fund (RLIF). From 55 you can make a
one-time transfer of up to 50% of your federally regulated locked-in holdings to an RRSP or RRIF
([OSFI: pension members' guide](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/pensions/understanding-your-pension-plan/pension-members-guide)).
It must happen within 60 days of the RLIF being established (the date money is first deposited), the 50% is measured on the
day of the withdrawal, and unused room is not carried forward
([OSFI: unlocking from RLIFs](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/supervision/pensions/administering-pension-plans/guidance-topic/unlocking-restricted-life-income-funds)).
OSFI's guide also describes small-balance unlocking from 55, when total federal locked-in holdings are under 50% of the
year's maximum pensionable earnings, and financial hardship unlocking.

## Planning with this in Glideworth

Glideworth applies the RRIF minimum to every RRIF automatically, using the CRA factors above, and RRIF withdrawals are
taxed as income in the Canadian tax estimate, with pension income splitting optimised. It uses the owner's age; the
spouse's age election is not modelled. It estimates the tax owed for each year and does not model withholding at source,
which is a prepayment of that tax. See [retirement planning in Canada](/planning/ca/) and
[withdrawal strategies](/features/withdrawal-strategies/) for the rest.

LIRA is an account type, but there is no separate LIF account type, and the planner does not apply a LIF maximum or any
unlocking rule. You can set an account's required distributions to "Canada RRIF minimum" to give it the LIF minimum. A plan
may still draw more from that account in a year than your LIF maximum allows, so check its yearly withdrawals against the
table for your province.
