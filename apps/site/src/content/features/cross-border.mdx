A retirement plan that crosses a border has to answer questions a single-country plan never asks. Which country
taxes the rent on the flat you kept? What happens to an ISA when you stop being a UK resident? Is the same
pension taxed twice? Glideworth models these mechanics in the engine. The web app has screens for the residency
timeline, citizenship and past residence, a country on each account and income, tax by country and a stay-or-move
comparison, and the API and MCP server expose the same things.

## How it works

Three things drive the model.

1. **Where you live, over time.** Each person has a residency timeline: dated moves to a country and region.
   Partners can live in different countries. A move part-way through a tax year splits it: each country taxes its
   share of the year, prorated by month. A milestone can also carry a "relocate" effect, for example "move home
   when I retire".
2. **Where each thing is.** Every account, property, debt and income has its own country, and it does not change
   when you move. A UK pension stays a UK pension; a flat in Manchester stays in the UK.
3. **Which country may tax what.** The country you live in taxes your worldwide income. A country where something
   is located may also tax it under its rules for non-residents. A treaty table says who keeps the right to tax
   each kind of income, and your country of residence gives a credit for tax already paid elsewhere.

## What it models

- **Residency timeline** per person, with split years prorated by month.
- **Source-country tax on non-residents:** rent, property gains, pensions and pay. The UK rules are the most
  detailed (non-resident landlord rules, capital gains rebasing, State Pension frozen where it is not uprated,
  temporary non-residence, inheritance tax long-term residence). US citizenship-based taxation, with the foreign
  earned income exclusion and foreign tax credit, is modelled at a basic level; other countries are basic.
- **Treaty relief by the credit method.** The UK-UAE treaty is implemented from the convention. UK-US, UK-Australia,
  UK-Canada, UK-Spain, UK-France and US-Canada are basic. Any other pair uses the credit method and says so with a
  warning.
- **Foreign wrappers:** how the country you live in treats a tax-free account from another country. The
  conservative default is to tax the growth on withdrawal.
- **Exit and arrival:** deemed disposition when leaving Canada or Australia (deferrable) and rebasing on arrival,
  and what happens to contribution rights, such as ISA and pension relief, when you leave.
- **The United Arab Emirates** is supported: no personal income, gains, dividend, wealth or inheritance tax, the
  end-of-service gratuity, savings schemes and GPSSA pensions at a basic level.

## Worked illustration: stay or move?

Suppose a UK couple considers three years in the UAE from 2034, with a flat in the UK left let out. Run the
comparison and Glideworth shows two projections side by side: lifetime tax by country and by reason (residence,
source, citizenship, relief, exit, return), the plan changes the move implies, and where the difference in final
net worth comes from. The reasons column keeps relief as its own negative figure, so you can see that nothing is
taxed twice. For any account or income there is a "how is this taxed and why" explanation for a given year, with
the non-resident rule, the treaty article and any warnings.

Through MCP, an assistant answers "should we move?" with `compare_stay_or_move`, keeps it with
`create_branch_from_template` (template `relocation`), or changes the plan with `propose_move`, which creates a
change set you approve.

## What it does not do

<Callout type="warning" title="An estimate of tax mechanics, not of your status">
  Glideworth applies **no statutory residence test**: no UK statutory residence test, no 183-day rule, no
  tie-breakers. You are resident where your timeline says. That is the most important limit on this page.
</Callout>

- **Split years are prorated by month.** UK split-year treatment, which keeps a full personal allowance, is not
  reproduced.
- **Treaty relief is assumed claimed.** Only UK-UAE is checked article by article; other pairs are basic.
- **Departure tax covers taxable investment accounts only,** valued at the start of the year you leave.
- **One cost basis per account;** after a deemed sale it is the market value everywhere.
- **Temporary non-residence covers capital gains only,** not pension lump sums taken abroad.
- **US citizens abroad** pay federal tax only in the model (no state tax), with one foreign-tax-credit basket and no carry-forward of unused credit (a simplification of the model).
- **Stay-or-move is deterministic** (two projections). For a chance of success, save the move as a branch and
  run [Monte Carlo](/features/monte-carlo/) on it.
- **UAE Corporate Tax on a business and VAT** are out of scope, and the gratuity is UAE-only.
- Voluntary pension contributions are not added to expenses automatically.
- **Single-country plans are unchanged:** a household that never moves gets byte-identical results to the engine
  before this feature.
