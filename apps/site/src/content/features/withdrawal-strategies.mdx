Retirement spending is two decisions. **Where the money comes from** is the drawdown order: which account to
empty first, which to leave to grow, and when each can be touched. **How much to take** is the withdrawal rule.
Glideworth models both, and can tell you what the rule says for this year.

## Drawdown order

By default a shortfall is met from cash, then taxable investments, crypto, tax-deferred accounts, health
accounts, tax-free accounts and finally real assets that you have marked as available to sell. You can reorder
categories or name individual accounts, draw within a category in list order or in proportion to balance, set a
cash buffer to keep, and set the date or age from which each account can be touched. Statutory rules still
apply: the 10% penalty on 401(k) and IRA withdrawals before 59½ where you turn it on, and the minimum access age
in countries that lock pensions until then.

## Withdrawal rules

A strategy sets how much to take from the portfolio each year. The rules available are:

- **Fixed amount**, inflation-adjusted or not.
- **Initial percent** (the "4% rule": a percentage of the starting portfolio, then adjusted for inflation) and
  **fixed percent** of the current portfolio.
- **1/N**, and **variable percentage withdrawal** (VPW), which rises with age.
- **Ratcheting**, which lifts spending after strong years, with a cool-down.
- **Clyatt 95%**, which never lets spending fall below 95% of the previous year.
- **Guyton-Klinger**, with a guardrail and an adjustment you set (20% and 10% by default).

Each rule can have a floor and a ceiling, and you choose whether other income reduces withdrawals or lifts
spending. **Flexible spending** is a simpler rule of the same family: when the portfolio is down by a set
fraction from its high, cut flexed spending by a set fraction, in steps or linearly, on all spending or just the
discretionary part.

## Guardrails that watch the risk

Percent-of-portfolio rules react to the portfolio. **Risk-based guardrails** react to the chance of success. You
set a target (80% by default) and a band around it (70% and 95%). If the chance of success leaves the band,
spending is cut or raised by a step. The planner works from one shared set of simulations rather than nesting
simulations inside simulations, and it turns the band into a withdrawal-rate band the engine applies year by
year, so you also get a plain answer: "what do I spend this year?"

## A worked illustration

The Guyton-Klinger rule in money terms, with illustrative numbers. A couple retires with $1,000,000 across a
401(k), Roth IRAs and a taxable brokerage account and spends $50,000, a 5% initial rate, with a 20% guardrail.
After a bad year, inflation lifts the spending to $51,000 while the portfolio has fallen to $760,000.

| | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Current withdrawal rate | $51,000 ÷ $760,000 = 6.7% |
| Cut-above rate (5% × 1.2) | 6.0% |
| Portfolio below which to cut | $51,000 ÷ 6% = $850,000 |
| Action | Cut spending by 10% to $45,900 |

The tool states this the other way round as well: the portfolio level below which to cut, the level above which to
raise, and the action now. Over MCP these are `guyton_klinger` and `evaluate_guardrails`; over REST,
`POST /api/v1/plans/{id}/insights/guyton-klinger` and `/insights/guardrails`.

## Tax strategies

Withdrawal order is also a tax decision. The planner models Roth conversions to a target bracket, an IRMAA tier,
an ACA income level or a fixed income, plus withdrawal shielding and gain harvesting for US plans. Other
countries have their own: for UK plans, drawdown order, using the personal allowance in gap years and the timing
of the tax-free lump sum. The tax-strategy comparison ranks candidates (or your own) by end net worth, lifetime tax or chance of
success, and a matrix compares strategies across those outcomes. See [solvers](/features/solvers/) and [tax estimates](/features/tax-estimates/).

## What it does not do

- **Rules model your intentions, not your behaviour.** A rule says what a disciplined household would do; real
  households may not.
- **No dedicated go-go and slow-go phases.** You can approximate them with flexible spending and milestones.
- **The tax optimiser has limited depth.** It ranks candidates it generates (or yours); it has no beam search or depth setting.
- **No Flex versus No-Flex pulse table.** Risk-based guardrails are compared instead.
- **Strategy and income-by-rate heatmaps are partial:** they run as background analysis jobs.
- **Survivor benefits are not added automatically** in survivor what-ifs.
- **Not advice.** A strategy is a model of a rule, and tax results are [estimates](/features/tax-estimates/).
