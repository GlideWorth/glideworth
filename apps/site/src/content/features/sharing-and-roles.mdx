Retirement planning is rarely a solo activity. Glideworth has two ways to include someone else: give them a
sign-in with a role, or give them a link to the results.

## Roles

One instance has one **owner**, created when you first set a passphrase, who can invite others. Invitations carry
a one-time token that is valid for seven days; the invited person chooses their own passphrase, so the owner never
learns it.

| Role | Can |
| --- | --- |
| Owner | Everything, including people, webhooks, connected apps and the audit log |
| Editor (a partner) | Read, simulate and write plans, finances, imports, share links and check-in settings, and manage their own API keys |
| Viewer (an adviser) | Read and simulate only. Every route and MCP tool that writes is refused or absent |

Roles are enforced where API scopes are: every route declares the scope it needs, and any credential's scopes
are capped by its person's role, whether it is a browser session, an API key or an OAuth token for an assistant.
Demoting or disabling someone takes effect at once on all their sessions and keys. Every write by a person, key or
connected app, and every refused write, is recorded in an audit log that the owner can read, and plan history
names who made each edit.

## Share links

A share link gives someone a view of a plan's **results** without an account. The link serves a snapshot of
computed results taken when you create it, not live access:

- the plan name and a summary: chance of success, retirement and financial-independence year, whether it is funded;
- the milestones reached (life-expectancy milestones are left out because they name a person); and
- a year-by-year table.

It never contains the household, people, accounts, balances, settings or your other plans. You choose how amounts
appear: **full** (in your currency), **percent** (every amount as a percentage of starting net worth, with no
currency), or **hidden** (amounts removed, leaving success rate, years and milestones). Links expire (30 days by
default, up to 365), can be revoked at once, can be refreshed to take a new snapshot, and show a view count. The
token is shown once, is 256 random bits and is stored only as a hash. Public reads are rate limited, return `404`
for unknown, expired and revoked links alike, and ask search engines not to index them.

## A worked illustration

You want your accountant to see how the plan is doing without seeing your balances. You create a link with privacy
**percent** and a 14-day expiry, and send it. They see "Chance of success 86%, funded to 2068, net worth by year
as a percentage of where you started", and no account names, no pounds. After the meeting you revoke it. All
figures here are illustrative.

If the accountant should poke at the model themselves, invite them as a **viewer** instead.

## Over the API and MCP

Share links are `POST /api/v1/plans/{id}/share-links` and a public `GET /api/v1/shared/{token}`. Invitations are
`POST /api/v1/users`. An assistant with write access can use `create_share_link`, `list_share_links` and
`revoke_share_link`. See [the API](/features/rest-api/) and [the MCP server](/features/mcp-server/).

## What it does not do

- **Roles are instance-wide,** not per household or per plan. Everyone with a sign-in sees the whole household.
  Per-person privacy inside a household is not modelled.
- **A share link is a snapshot.** It is not a live dashboard; refresh it to update it.
- **A share link is not an API credential.** It opens no other route.
- **No advisor workspace:** there is no multi-client view, no branding and no client management for professionals.
- **No single sign-on** or directory integration.
- **Two-factor sign-in is planned for the hosted service,** not built. Self-hosted sign-in is a passphrase.
  See [security](/security/).
