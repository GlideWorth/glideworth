This is the complete list, taken from the project's status document and checked against the code on 2026-11-19.
Read it before you rely on a number.

<Callout type="warning" title="Estimator, not advice">
  Treat every output as a model of your assumptions. Glideworth does not know your circumstances, your tax residence in
  detail or your risk tolerance. Talk to a regulated adviser before acting on a projection.
</Callout>

## Not modelled

- Monthly or daily resolution. The engine steps by year, so nothing within a year is seen.
- Moving country. The household lives in one country (and, in the US, one state) for the whole plan. There is no
  change of residence, so no departure or exit tax, no rule for returning after a short time abroad, no State Pension
  frozen abroad and no change in UK inheritance tax scope after leaving. Accounts, property and income can still be in
  another country.
- Randomised lifespan and care-cost shocks inside the Monte Carlo (you set a life expectancy; a care scenario is a
  what-if).
- Account aggregation, by design, and live sync with budgeting apps.
- A server-side PDF.
- The municipal and Treasury bond-income split.
- The US earned income credit and education credits.
- Means-tested benefits (UK Universal Credit, Pension Credit).
- Loss harvesting and specific-lot selling.
- End-to-end encrypted sync, and per-person privacy within a household.
- Changes in the law that are not already legislated, and behaviour (panic selling, windfalls, a marriage or divorce you
  have not planned).
- Survivor pensions beyond what you enter.
- Investment fees beyond the percentage or amount you set.

## Partial

US federal tax; Canada CPP, OAS and GIS; Germany, the Netherlands, France, Spain, Israel, Ireland and New Zealand (a
basic level); debt paydown (a flow pays debts by avalanche or snowball, with no side-by-side comparison); datasets;
drawdown-order graphs; the Flex versus No-Flex table; the depth of the tax optimiser; strategy and income-by-rate heatmaps; table export (four tables, not any plot); reports (browser print
only); share links; re-run on plan change (on request, not on every edit); household collaboration (roles are
instance-wide); onboarding data-quality checks; scheduled re-runs (no email; use webhooks); open test vectors (no
published validation report).

## Tax tables and years

Later years carry the last known rates forward and index thresholds with inflation, which is wrong whenever the rules
change. Someone has to add each new year each spring. The years in the tables are listed on
[Tax settings, countries and currency](/docs/planning/tax-and-currency/). The US 2017-law sunset variant is an
estimate (the 2017 law scaled by 1.33), not law.

Each of the {facts.taxCountryCount} countries' full simplification list is shown with your tax results. The main ones follow. Each "no" or "not" describes
what Glideworth's model leaves out, not what the law says; where the law allows something the model omits, the item says so.

- **UK:** the model does not carry forward unused pension annual allowance (UK law allows it from the three previous tax years); Class 1 employee and Class 4 National Insurance only;
  one pair of capital gains rates for all assets; no blind person's or married couple's allowance, trading or property
  allowance, or Gift Aid band extension; student-loan interest ignored.
- **US:** itemised deductions are what you enter (the SALT cap is not applied for you); AMT adds back only the standard
  deduction; QBI is simplified; credits are the child tax credit and the ACA premium credit only; states start from
  federal AGI with no state-specific additions or subtractions; local tax only for the cities in the table.
- **Canada:** no AMT; GIS is an amount you enter; provincial low-income reductions and the Quebec return's own amounts
  are not modelled.
- **Australia:** residents only; no SAPTO, HELP repayments or Medicare levy surcharge; Medicare levy uses single
  thresholds; the model does not carry forward an unused concessional cap (the law allows it in some cases) and applies no transfer balance cap.
- **Germany:** statutory health insurance at the average supplementary rate; no Kindergeld comparison; investments
  assumed to be equity funds (30 percent partial exemption, switchable) and no Vorabpauschale; Riester allowance not
  modelled; tax not rounded to whole euros; self-employed pay no social contributions.
- **Netherlands:** box 3 uses the 2026 deemed returns (the savings and debt rates are provisional) without the
  actual-return counter-evidence route; pension contributions are deducted without checking the jaarruimte; no
  mortgage-interest or own-home rules; box 2 only for income you tag as such.
- **France:** salaries are gross with employee pension contributions and CSG/CRDS (other contributions not modelled);
  PEA and assurance-vie are assumed held 5 and 8 years (switchable); the PER is available from 64 (the 2025 suspension
  is not modelled); no high-income contribution or IFI.
- **Spain:** only Madrid, Cataluña, Andalucía and Valencia have their own scales (others use the state scale);
  individual returns only; no regional minimums or deductions; wealth tax off by default.
- **Israel:** capital gains are taxed on the nominal gain (Israel taxes only the real gain), so long-held assets are
  over-taxed; pensions are taxed in full because the partial exemption could not be confirmed from an official source;
  only the basic credit points.
- **Ireland:** capital gains tax for 2026 averages the 33 percent and 31 percent rates by days (the rate fell on 7 October
  2026; the model has no disposal dates), and is 31 percent from 2027; no
  exit tax on funds; no reduced USC rates for over-70s; the 25 percent tax-free lump sum is applied only if you set a
  tax-free withdrawal share.
- **New Zealand:** no capital gains tax at all (no bright-line or FIF rules); dividends assumed fully imputed; no
  independent earner tax credit.
- **United Arab Emirates:** the gratuity's daily wage (monthly basic divided by 30) is Glideworth's convention, and
  whether the two-year cap uses basic or total wage is not settled on the government portal (basic is used); the DEWS
  voluntary-savings cap and GPSSA eligibility ages could not be confirmed and are not modelled.
- **Everywhere:** gains are realised pro rata to cost basis and treated as long-term; losses are never realised.

Figures that could not be confirmed or are provisional: Israeli brackets, credit points, capital-income rates and
National Insurance rates could not be confirmed from the Tax Authority or National Insurance Institute; some US
states' figures (all of Idaho, Kansas, Louisiana, Michigan, New Hampshire, Ohio, Rhode Island and Wisconsin, and
some of nine others) and Quebec's brackets could not be confirmed from the state's or province's own site, and those
results say so; Australian Medicare levy low-income thresholds are the 2024-25 values; the 2026 Dutch box 3 deemed returns; the Spanish 2025 scales carried forward; the UK State
Pension uprating list comes from a government page that may be dated. No preset has been checked against every
official calculator for every case.

## Assets and income in another country

- Residence is the household's country, for everyone, for the whole plan: no statutory residence test, no 183-day
  rules, and treaty relief assumed claimed.
- Only UK-UAE is implemented article by article. UK-US, UK-Australia, UK-Canada, UK-Spain, UK-France and US-Canada are
  basic. Other pairs use the credit method with a warning.
- Non-resident rules are detailed for the UK and basic elsewhere (no withholding on interest or dividends; resident
  rates are used for non-residents).
- While you live outside a wrapper's country, the model stops contributions to it (ISA, UK pensions, TFSA, RRSP). It
  does not model the UK relief on pension contributions of up to 3,600 pounds a year in the five tax years after
  leaving, which UK law allows.
- US citizens abroad pay federal tax only, with one foreign-tax-credit basket in the model and no carry-forward of
  unused credit.
- The bracket and marginal-rate analytics on the Taxes page describe the household country's calculation only.
  Planned real assets and plan-level account overrides have no country field in the plan editor. The gratuity is
  UAE-only.

## Running it yourself

Self-hosted means you are responsible: back up the database, keep the server up to date, and keep the port off the
public internet unless you have set a passphrase, a setup token and TLS.
