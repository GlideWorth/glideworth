The United States is, with the United Kingdom, the most detailed model in Glideworth. Federal and payroll tax
cover 2026 and 2027 under the law as amended by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), using the figures the IRS,
the Social Security Administration and Medicare have published. Every state and DC are modelled, and five cities. Glideworth carries {facts.simplificationCount('us-federal')} written simplifications for the
federal model alone, and shows them beside your tax results.

Plans use calendar years. Years after 2027 index the 2027 figures with the plan's threshold policy, starting from
2027 prices; a few thresholds fixed in statute, such as the net investment income tax and Social Security
benefit-taxation thresholds, stay fixed.

## Federal income tax, 2027

The IRS published the 2027 figures in Rev. Proc. 2026-35 on 5 November 2026.

| | Single | Married filing jointly |
|---|---|---|
| Brackets | 10% to $12,800; 12% to $52,025; 22% to $109,125; 24% to $208,325; 32% to $264,550; 35% to $661,375; 37% above | 10% to $25,600; 12% to $104,050; 22% to $218,250; 24% to $416,650; 32% to $529,100; 35% to $793,650; 37% above |
| Standard deduction | $16,600 | $33,200 |
| Extra deduction at 65 or over | $2,100 | $1,700 each |
| 0% rate on gains and qualified dividends, up to taxable income of | $51,050 | $102,100 |
| 15% rate up to | $563,200 | $633,600 |

Head of household, married filing separately and qualifying surviving spouse have their own rows. The senior
deduction of $6,000 is available from 2025 to 2028 and phases out at 6% above $75,000 of income ($150,000 joint);
married couples must file jointly to claim it. The net investment income tax is 3.8% above $200,000 ($250,000
joint). The child tax credit is $2,200 per child in 2026 and $2,300 in 2027 ($1,700 and $1,800 of it refundable),
phasing out above $200,000 ($400,000 joint). The alternative minimum tax exemption phases out at 50 cents per
dollar from 2026. Social Security benefit taxation (the 50% and 85% tiers), IRMAA surcharges and the ACA premium
tax credit are modelled too. The enhanced ACA credits expired after 2025, so there is no credit above 400% of the
poverty line; a setting turns them back on if you want to test that.

## Payroll tax and Social Security

Employee Social Security tax is 6.2% on wages up to $184,500 in 2026 and $192,300 in 2027, Medicare 1.45% on all wages, and the
Additional Medicare Tax 0.9% above $200,000 ($250,000 joint). Self-employment tax is included. Pre-tax 401(k)
deferrals do not reduce these taxes, as the law says.

For Social Security benefits, Glideworth can estimate the benefit from an earnings record (primary insurance
amount, claiming age, the earnings test, spousal and survivor benefits), or you can enter an amount with a
cost-of-living adjustment. Up to 85% of the benefit can be taxable, using the statutory thresholds
($25,000 and $34,000 single, $32,000 and $44,000 joint).

## Accounts, limits and access

Glideworth knows 401(k), Roth 401(k), 403(b), 457(b), 401(a), solo 401(k), TSP, traditional, Roth, SEP, SIMPLE and
inherited IRAs, HSA, 529 and Coverdell accounts, and taxable brokerage accounts. Limits in the tables (2026, then
2027):

- Elective deferrals to a 401(k) or similar plan: $24,500 / $25,500, plus a catch-up from age 50 of $8,000 /
  $8,500, or $11,250 / $11,750 at ages 60 to 63.
- IRA: $7,500 in both years, plus a $1,100 catch-up from age 50.
- SIMPLE: $17,000 / $17,500, plus $4,000 catch-up, or $5,250 at ages 60 to 63. SEP or solo total: $72,000 /
  $75,000.
- HSA: $4,400 / $4,500 for self-only cover, $8,750 / $9,000 for family cover, plus $1,000 from age 55.

Early withdrawals before 59 and a half carry a 10% penalty unless the account setting says otherwise; 72(t)
payments are penalty-free. Non-qualified HSA withdrawals before 65 carry a 20% additional tax, and non-qualified
529 earnings 10%. Roth earnings are qualified after 59 and a half and five tax years from the first
contribution. Required minimum distributions follow the SECURE 2.0 starting age (73 for people born 1951 to 1959,
75 from 1960) and the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table. Roth conversions and tax-strategy comparison are available.
Main-home sale gains are excluded up to $250,000 ($500,000 joint). The federal estate tax exclusion is $15,000,000 in 2026 and $15,480,000 in 2027.

## State and city tax

All 50 states and DC use their 2026 rates and brackets. Each state's figures were checked on 16 and 19 November
2026 against that state's revenue department, statute or tax-year forms, which the tax results cite. Idaho's
official sites could not be opened, so none of its figures is confirmed; nine other states (Arizona, Colorado,
Louisiana, Maine, Montana, Ohio, Rhode Island, South Carolina and Vermont) have one or two figures that are not yet
published or could not be confirmed. Each of those states' results says which figures are not confirmed from an
official source. Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas and Wyoming levy no income
tax, and Washington taxes only long-term capital gains.

Most states start from federal adjusted gross income; Colorado, Iowa, Montana and North Dakota start from federal
taxable income, and New Mexico and DC use the federal standard deduction. State rules for retirement income are
modelled where the state has them: Social Security exemptions with their age and income tests, pension and IRA
exclusions with their income tests, extra amounts at 65, and the deductions for federal income tax in Alabama and
Missouri. Exclusions that apply only to government or military pensions are not modelled. California's state
disability insurance (1.3% of wages in 2026, 1.4% in 2027) is included in the California row. City income tax is
modelled for New York City, Philadelphia, Wilmington (Delaware), Detroit and Newark (which levies nothing on
residents). Thirty-two state rows carry a noted approximation, for example for a married-couple threshold or the
scope of a pension exclusion; check them before relying on a figure for a particular return.

## Worked example

<Callout type="note" title="Illustrative">
  Made-up figures, to show the arithmetic with the 2026 tables. This is not a forecast or advice.
</Callout>

A single person earns $90,000 in wages in 2026, with no pre-tax deferrals and no itemised deductions.

- Taxable income: $90,000 − $16,100 standard deduction = $73,900.
- Federal income tax: 10% × $12,400 = $1,240; 12% × ($50,400 − $12,400 = $38,000) = $4,560; 22% × ($73,900 −
  $50,400 = $23,500) = $5,170. Total **$10,970**.
- Payroll tax: Social Security 6.2% × $90,000 = $5,580; Medicare 1.45% × $90,000 = $1,305. Total **$6,885**.
- Federal and payroll tax together: $10,970 + $6,885 = **$17,855**. State and city tax would come on top.

## Historical data for simulations

Glideworth's default for US households is US market history from 1928 to 2025: the S&P 500, 10-year Treasury
bonds, Treasury bills and CPI-U, in dollars (from Damodaran's published series; BLS CPI is public domain). The
longer series from 1871 (Shiller's data) is no longer bundled because it has no explicit licence; self-hosters can
download it for their own use. Datasets are not updated automatically, and crypto has no history, so it is sampled from a
distribution.

## What to treat with care

- The "TCJA sunset" setting, which models the 2017 tax law returning, is an estimate: the 2017 brackets and
  deductions multiplied by 1.33, with personal exemptions restored. It is not law.
- Itemised deductions are what you enter. The SALT cap is not applied for you.
- The AMT adds back only the standard and senior deductions, and the QBI deduction treats self-employment income as a
  specified service business.
- IRMAA is estimated from income two years earlier (or the latest year early in the plan), using the 2026 and
  2027 tiers, indexed after 2027 like other thresholds.
- The household lives in one state for the whole year.
- Dollar thresholds in later years are projected, so a figure for 2035 is a projection, not a published rate.
