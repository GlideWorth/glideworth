A retirement projection is only as good as its tax figures. Tax decides how much of a pension you
keep, which account to draw from first, and whether a plan that looks comfortable before tax still
works after it. So Glideworth estimates tax every year of a plan, for every person in the household,
in {facts.taxCountryCount} countries: the United Kingdom, the United States (federal, payroll, all fifty states and
DC, and some cities), Canada, Australia, Germany, the Netherlands, France, Spain, Israel, Ireland,
New Zealand and the United Arab Emirates. Flat and custom rates cover everywhere else.

Every one of those is an **estimate**, and we say so on purpose.

## Every country comes with a list of simplifications

Tax codes are long, and a planner that claims to model all of one is either very narrow or not
telling you something. Glideworth takes the other route: each country's model ships with a written
list of what it does not do, kept in the code next to the rules themselves and shown beside your tax
results. A few examples from that list today:

- **United Kingdom:** National Insurance covers Class 1 employee and Class 4 contributions only; the
  pension annual allowance is tapered, but the model does not carry forward unused allowance from the three
  previous tax years, as UK law allows; one basic and one higher capital gains rate apply to all assets.
- **United States:** the SALT cap and the phase-outs of itemised deductions are not applied
  automatically, and there is no earned income credit or education credits.
- **Everywhere:** gains are realised in proportion to cost basis when a taxable account is drawn, all
  of them are treated as long-term, and losses are never harvested.

None of these is hidden in a footnote. If a simplification matters to your situation, you can see it
next to the number it affects and adjust the plan, or enter the amount yourself.

## Tables are dated, and later years are carried forward

Each country's tables are for stated tax years: for example 2025/26 and 2026/27 in the UK, with the
announced 2027/28 and 2029/30 changes, and 2026 for the US federal rules. Years after the last table
carry the latest rates forward and index thresholds with inflation, or keep them frozen where the
law freezes them. That is a reasonable default, and it is wrong whenever the law changes. Tables
have to be updated every year, and each update will be listed in the [changelog](/changelog/).

<Callout type="note" title="Estimates, not advice">
  Glideworth is planning software. Its tax figures help you compare choices under your own
  assumptions; they are not a tax return and not tax advice.
</Callout>

## Why tell you at all

Because a plan you cannot inspect is a plan you have to trust. Glideworth is open source, so the
rules are readable, and the list of what each model leaves out is the honest summary of those rules.
When you know where an estimate is thin, you know where to be cautious, where to leave a margin, and
when to ask a professional.
