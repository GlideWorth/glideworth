**Before you start:** a main plan you are happy with.

## Versions and undo

Every change to a plan is a new version, with who made it (you, an API key or an MCP client). History is
append-only. To undo, restore an older version: that writes the old content as a **new** version, so nothing is
lost. In the Plan editor, undo is one step back through the history.

## Branches

A branch is a plan that stores only its differences from a parent. It follows the parent when the parent changes,
and can be merged back or promoted.

<Steps>
1. Open **Plans**, or the Plan editor's **What-if** section.
2. Create a branch and give it a name ("Name for the new branch"), for example "Retire three years earlier".
3. Edit the branch freely. The main plan is untouched.
4. Open **Compare** to see both side by side. Merge it back if you like the result.
</Steps>

New plans can also start from a template or a copy: on the **Plans** page, "New plan" asks for a "Starting point",
a "Template" or a "Plan to copy". Plans can be archived and unarchived, and one is marked as the main plan.

## Compare

**Compare** shows plans on net worth at end, peak net worth, retirement, financial independence, first shortfall,
unfunded spending, lifetime spending, lifetime taxes, average savings rate and first withdrawal rate. "Where the
difference comes from" replays each individual edit alone to say how much of the outcome change it explains, plus
the interaction left over.

## What-if templates

Templates turn a few parameters into a plan change you can preview before saving. In the app they appear as
"Life-event templates". The templates are: market crash, job loss, career break, early retirement, downsizing,
child, inheritance, long-term care and survivor. A preview shows what would change and the before and
after outcome, and stores nothing. Saving one as a branch leaves the parent untouched.

## Survivor scenarios

For a couple, "If one of you dies first" shows what happens if each partner dies at 70, 80 and 90, or ages you
choose, with before and after outcomes. A one-person household gets a note, not an empty list.

<Callout type="warning" title="Survivor benefits are not added automatically">
  Assets pass to the survivor and filing status changes, but survivor pensions beyond what you enter are not
  added. The scenario says so.
</Callout>

## What this does not do

- A branch does not copy household balances: plans that start now read your current finances.
- Merging does not silently overwrite. If something changed underneath, you are told.
