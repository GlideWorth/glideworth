Before a full planner, many people use a retirement calculator or a spreadsheet, and many keep using them. This
page compares Glideworth with three web calculators (FIRECalc, cFIREsim and FI Calc), with the Bogleheads
variable percentage withdrawal (VPW) worksheet, and with a spreadsheet you build yourself. Every statement about
the three web calculators links to its page and the date we read it, 16 November 2026.

## Who each suits

**A calculator is the better choice if** you have one question and want an answer in minutes. FIRECalc tests a
spending plan against every historical start year since 1871 and reports how often it survived
([FIRECalc](https://www.firecalc.com/), read 16 Nov 2026). cFIREsim offers a range of spending rules, including
percent of portfolio, VPW, Hebeler Autopilot, Guyton-Klinger and Variable CAPE, and an "investigate" mode that
finds the highest starting spending for a target success rate
([cFIREsim](https://www.cfiresim.com/), 16 Nov 2026). FI Calc backtests withdrawal strategies such as constant
dollar, percent of portfolio, VPW, Guyton-Klinger, the 95% rule and Vanguard dynamic spending, and reports
success rate, spending volatility and a per-simulation table ([FI Calc](https://ficalc.app/), 16 Nov 2026).
Glideworth needs you to describe a household before it answers anything.

**A spreadsheet you build is the better choice if** you want every formula in front of you and the freedom to
model something unusual, and you are willing to maintain it. Nothing hides in a spreadsheet you wrote, and you
can change any assumption in a cell.

**Glideworth may suit you better if** the question is bigger than "does this withdrawal rate survive?": you want
accounts, income, spending and milestones over decades in one plan, with tax estimated each year for each person,
and the result shown as a year-by-year ledger. It also suits you if you want to branch a plan to compare changes,
or reach the plan from scripts or an AI assistant.

### Where the calculators are stronger

- **Speed to an answer.** You do not describe a household first.
- **Rule variety on the page.** cFIREsim and FI Calc list several withdrawal rules that Glideworth does not
  list by name, such as Hebeler Autopilot, Variable CAPE and Vanguard dynamic spending. Glideworth has guardrails
  and other rules, but not these.
- **More recent data on the page.** FIRECalc states data through 1 January 2026 and cFIREsim states
  1 August 2026. Glideworth's bundled US data ends in 2025 and is not updated automatically.
- **No set-up.** They run in a browser.

### Where Glideworth does more

None of the three calculators describes tax modelling on the pages we read, or a household with several
accounts and income streams over time. That is a gap in what those pages say, not proof the tools lack
it. Glideworth also keeps a year-by-year ledger of every money movement, so you can see why a plan fails, where
the money went in a year, and what edit explains a difference between two plans.

### And a spreadsheet you build

A spreadsheet is the most flexible option and the cheapest to start. The cost shows later: you maintain the tax
tables and update them when the law changes, build any Monte Carlo yourself, and carry the risk that a formula
error goes unnoticed. Glideworth has tests for its engine and its tax tables, a list of what each country's
estimate leaves out, and an audit that flags common mistakes in a plan, but it is not error-free either.

## Price

The three calculators' pages we read state no price for FIRECalc or cFIREsim; FI Calc says it is supported by
donations. cFIREsim's page also offers a "try it free" link that refers to a separate product, so check each
before you rely on it being free. Glideworth is free to self-host with every feature. The hosted service,

## Moving over from a spreadsheet or calculator

There is no importer for a calculator or for a personal spreadsheet. You enter your household, accounts,
income, spending and milestones, through the web app, the API or an AI assistant over MCP. If your spreadsheet
holds balances in a layout like a bank or budgeting export, Glideworth's generic CSV import may read it; check
the dry-run report before you apply anything. If you have been using a calculator with a spending rule, you can
set the matching rule in Glideworth if it is among those listed above, and compare the two answers before you
trust either.

Expect different results. Glideworth's default history is US 1928 to 2025, and it models returns, inflation and
tax together in one run.
