This walks you from a fresh install to a first result. The figures in the app are your own; nothing here
is advice.

**Before you start:** Glideworth running, either self-hosted ([install with Docker](/docs/self-hosting/docker/))
or the [hosted service](/docs/hosted/hosted-service/). You need rough balances for your
accounts, your yearly pay before tax, your yearly spending and the age you would like to stop working.

## 1. Sign in

<Steps>
1. Open the address of your instance (`http://localhost:8787` for a local Docker install).
2. On a new database the app asks you to choose a passphrase of 12 or more characters. If the server has
   `GLIDEWORTH_SETUP_TOKEN` set, it also asks for that token. You become the owner and are signed in.
3. There is no email, so there is no "forgot password" link. See
   [Troubleshooting](/docs/self-hosting/troubleshooting/) for the reset command.
</Steps>

## 2. Choose how to start

The Welcome page offers three starting points:

- **Start from scratch**, with the button "Begin setup": a short guided setup.
- **Explore a sample household**: three worked examples with invented people and numbers. "Avery & Jordan" is
  a US couple, "Sam" a single UK household, and "Noor & Ellis" a UAE couple with a UK pension.
  Loading a sample replaces your data if you already have some, and the app asks first.
- **Bring your data**: "Import from ProjectionLab" or "Import CSV files". See [Importing](/docs/importing/projectionlab/).

## 3. Answer the six setup questions

"Begin setup" has six steps. A summary on the right ("So far") shows your household, net worth, income,
spending and retirement as you go.

<Steps>
1. **Where you live.** Choose the country of tax residence, a region if the country has them, the currency,
   and the number and date format. This sets the currency, tax year and account types.
2. **Who is in the plan.** Your first name (only used for labels) and birth month. Tick "I am planning with
   a partner" to add a second person.
3. **What you have.** Add accounts: a type, a name, a balance and an owner. A few balances are enough.
4. **What you earn.** Your salary per year, before tax, and your partner's if there is one.
5. **What you spend.** "Everyday essentials" and "Travel and fun" (spending you could trim), entered per
   week, month or year.
6. **Your retirement goal.** "Retire at age", "Plan until age", and a plan name.
</Steps>

Choose the finish button on the last step. The app creates your household and your first plan and says
the plan is ready.

## 4. Read the first result

<Steps>
1. Open **Results** in the sidebar. You see net worth year by year, your milestone dates and any year money
   runs short. Use the "Today's money" and "Future money" switch in the top bar.
2. Open **Chance of success** and run it. Read the headline first, then the panels "When failed trials run
   short" and "Early returns vs success".
3. Open **Insights** and ask a solver question, such as the most you can spend at a target chance of success.
4. Open the **Plan editor** to refine: add milestones, flows, a mortgage or other income.
</Steps>

<Callout type="note" title="It starts rough on purpose">
  The setup is deliberately quick. Spend your time in the Plan editor, where each section (Income,
  Expenses, Flows, Accounts, Property and debts, Withdrawals, Assumptions, Taxes) lets you add the detail
  that matters for you. See [Build a plan](/docs/planning/build-a-plan/).
</Callout>

## What this does not do

It does not check your inputs against your real statements, and the first result uses default
assumptions. Set the tax mode to estimate for your country (see
[Tax settings and currency](/docs/planning/tax-and-currency/)) before you rely on a figure.
