The UAE levies no personal income tax, so there is no tax table to read; the work is in the end-of-service benefits
and savings schemes, which is where an expatriate's retirement money often sits. The UAE model has
{facts.simplificationCount('ae')} written simplifications and the tax year is 2026 (calendar year). The currency is the dirham (AED),
pegged to the US dollar at 3.6725 per dollar.

## What is modelled

- **No personal tax.** The UAE does not tax individuals' income, capital gains, dividends, interest, wealth or
  inheritance. Corporate Tax can apply to a natural person who runs a business or freelance licence with turnover above
  AED 1,000,000 a year; wages, personal investment income and unlicensed real-estate income are not business
  activities. That tax and VAT are out of scope: model business tax as an expense if it applies.
- **End-of-service gratuity** for expatriate private-sector employees: nothing under one year of service; 21 days of
  basic wage for each of the first five years and 30 days for each year after, pro rata for part years once one year is
  completed; capped at two years of wage. You set it on the salary, and it is paid in the month the salary ends.
- **DEWS** (DIFC Employee Workplace Savings) and the **voluntary savings scheme** (Cabinet Resolution No. 96 of 2023)
  replace the gratuity for employers who join. Employer contributions are 5.83% of basic wage for the first five years of
  service and 8.33% after. The savings scheme allows voluntary employee top-ups of up to 25% of wage. Enter employer contributions as a flow
  into the account, and do not also set a gratuity on that salary.
- **GPSSA** (Federal Decree-Law No. 57 of 2023, for UAE nationals whose first job started on or after 31 October 2023):
  contributions of 11% by the insured and 15% by the employer (the government bears 2.5% of the private employer share
  below AED 20,000 a month), a salary ceiling of AED 70,000 (private sector), and a pension of 2.67% of the calculation
  salary for each year up to 30 years, then 4%, to a maximum of 100%, on the average of the last six years. It is set up as a
  defined-benefit pension on the salary.
- **No state pension for expatriates.**

## Worked example

<Callout type="note" title="Illustrative">
  Made-up figures, to show the arithmetic of the gratuity rule. It is not a forecast or advice, and it applies to an
  expatriate private-sector employee.
</Callout>

An employee with a final monthly basic wage of AED 10,000 leaves after 8 years of continuous service.

- Daily wage: AED 10,000 ÷ 30 = AED 333.33 (the 30-day month is Glideworth's convention).
- Days of pay: 5 years × 21 = 105, plus 3 years × 30 = 90, so 195 days.
- Gratuity: 195 × AED 333.33 = **AED 65,000**.
- Cap: 2 years of basic wage = 24 × AED 10,000 = AED 240,000, which is not reached.

## Historical data for simulations

No UAE market history is bundled. Because the dirham is pegged to the US dollar, UAE households are tested against
US history (S&P 500, 10-year Treasuries, T-bills and CPI-U, 1928 to 2025, in dollars).

## What to treat with care

- The gratuity's daily wage (monthly basic ÷ 30) is Glideworth's convention. The government portal gives the rule in
  days of wage but no formula; basic annual wage ÷ 365 gives about 1.4% less.
- Whether the two-year cap uses basic or total wage is not settled on the government portal. Glideworth uses basic wage.
- The DEWS voluntary-savings cap and the GPSSA eligibility ages could not be confirmed, and are not modelled.
- The gratuity is a UAE-only feature. A gratuity or bonus paid after you move abroad is taxed where you then live.
