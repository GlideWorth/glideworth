Tax decides how much of a pension you keep, which account to draw from first and whether a plan that looks
comfortable before tax still works after it. Glideworth estimates tax for every person in the household, every
year of the plan, in {facts.taxCountryCount} countries: {facts.taxCountries.map((c) => c.name).join(', ')}. Flat
and custom bracket tables cover everywhere else.

Every figure is an **estimate**. It is not a tax return and not tax advice, and it is labelled that way in the
app and the API.

## What a country's tax model covers

Each country is a plug-in to the engine. A plug-in knows the rules for that country's tax system and for the
accounts that live in it. In general that means:

- Income tax by band, allowances and credits, for each person, with joint filing or independent taxation as the
  country does it.
- Social contributions and levies where they apply (National Insurance, FICA and Medicare, CSG and CRDS, ACC and
  so on), with the limits the country sets.
- Tax on dividends, interest and capital gains, with wrapper rules such as ISAs, Roth accounts, TFSAs, PEA or
  KiwiSaver.
- Statutory limits that come from the account's country: contribution limits, pension access ages, home-sale
  exclusions and required minimum distributions. A wrapper that cannot legally be drawn early stays locked until
  its access age unless you model a penalty.
- Estate tax and net legacy for the US and the UK (inheritance tax), and wealth tax as a custom or an opt-in
  rule in some countries.

The United States covers federal income tax, payroll tax, all 50 states and DC, and a subset of cities. Detail differs by country: Germany, the Netherlands, France, Spain, Israel, Ireland and New Zealand are
estimated at a basic level, and parts of the US and Canadian models (for example the US alternative minimum tax and
Canada's Guaranteed Income Supplement) are partial.

## Tax years are stated

Tables are for stated tax years, shown with the results:

| Country | Years in the tables |
| --- | --- |
| United Kingdom | 2025/26 and 2026/27, with announced changes for 2027/28 and 2029/30 |
| United States | 2026 federal, payroll and states; a "2017 law returns" what-if is itself an estimate |
| Canada | 2026 |
| Australia | 2025-26, 2026-27 and legislated 2027-28 |
| Germany, Netherlands, Israel, Ireland | 2026 |
| New Zealand | 2026-27 |
| France | 2026 finance law (taxes 2025 income), 2026 social rates |
| Spain | 2025 income-tax scales, 2026 social security |
| United Arab Emirates | 2026 |

Years after the last table carry the latest rates forward and index thresholds with inflation, or keep them
frozen where the law freezes them. That is a reasonable default and it is wrong whenever the law changes. Someone
has to add the new year every spring, and tax-table updates will be listed in the [changelog](/changelog/).

## Every estimate says what it leaves out

Each country's model ships with a written list of simplifications, kept in the code next to the rules and shown
beside your tax results. Highlights from today's lists:

- **United States:** the SALT cap and itemised-deduction phase-outs are not applied automatically, the alternative
  minimum tax adds back only the standard deduction, and the earned income and education credits are not
  modelled.
- **United Kingdom:** the model does not carry forward unused pension annual allowance (UK law allows carry-forward
  from the three previous tax years), National Insurance covers Class 1 employee and Class 4 only, and one pair of capital gains rates applies to all assets.
- **Canada:** no alternative minimum tax, and the Guaranteed Income Supplement is an amount you enter.
- **Australia:** no seniors offset, HELP repayments or Medicare levy surcharge.
- **Everywhere:** gains are realised in proportion to cost basis and treated as long-term, and losses are never
  harvested.

## Figures we could not confirm

Where a figure could not be confirmed from an official source, it is marked provisional in the code and listed in
the project's status document. Today that includes the Israeli exemption for some pension annuities (pensions are
taxed in full), the Netherlands box 3 deemed returns for savings and debts, and Spain's 2025 scales carried forward. No country preset has been checked line by line against an
official calculator for every case; the models are tested against worked examples, and anything you will act on
should be checked.

<Callout type="warning" title="Estimates, not advice">
  Glideworth shows the consequences of the assumptions you enter. It does not know your full circumstances, and it
  is not a substitute for a tax return or a regulated adviser.
</Callout>

## What it does not do

- **No means-tested benefits,** and no municipal or Treasury bond-income split.
- **No automatic law updates.** Future rule changes are your assumption until a table is added.
- **No checked-against-official-calculator guarantee** for every case, as above.
- **Local taxes** exist only for the US cities in the table, not for every municipality anywhere.
